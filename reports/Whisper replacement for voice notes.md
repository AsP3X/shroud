# Upgrade Whisper rather than replace it

Shroud should upgrade inside the Whisper family and should not replace it. No open model documented through 27 October 2026 keeps all thirty voice-note languages, identifies the language from the audio, punctuates in that same decode, and runs as a weights download on the iOS, Android, and web clients the app already ships. The best alternative that was actually evidenced end to end is Meta’s Omnilingual ASR CTC-300M: Apache-2.0 weights, every required language code, a November 2025 character-error comparison in which the 300M CTC beats Whisper small, and a sherpa-onnx int8 file of about 348 MiB. A runtime-and-coverage checklist can mark that checkpoint as surviving. The product cannot. The phone model emits spoken-form text with no punctuation or capitalization, does not take a language, and has no documented language identification, so a lower average error rate does not replace the punctuated, language-tagged note Whisper already produces. The nearest model that does identify language, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, still misses Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian, loses to Whisper large-v3 on its own thirty-language FLEURS average, and has no official path through WhisperKit, whisper.cpp, and transformers.js. If the shipping default is still about Whisper small, the evidenced accuracy move is a larger Whisper checkpoint. large-v3-turbo captures most of that gain on high-resource languages and is the phone-sized file; it is not equivalent to large-v3 on Thai or, in the one small sample that measured it, on Hindi.

## Specialists that beat Whisper do it by dropping languages

The public boards that make 2025–2026 models look like Whisper replacements are not a thirty-language test. The March 2026 Open ASR Leaderboard paper scores short-form English on one track and, on its multilingual track, only German, French, Italian, Spanish, and Portuguese. Word error is computed after punctuation and casing are stripped, and the speed figure is inverse real-time factor on one A100 at a large batch, which the authors say does not transfer to other hardware ([Srivastav et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)). On that English average, Whisper large-v3 sits at **7.44% WER and RTFx 146**. Several open models beat it there: Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 at **6.32% and RTFx 3330** across 25 languages, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B at **5.76% and RTFx 148**, Canary-1B-v2 at **7.15%**, and Voxtral Small 24B at **6.62%**. On the five-language track the same paper has Voxtral Small 24B at **3.70**, Omnilingual ASR LLM 7B v2 at **4.39**, Canary-1B-v2 at **4.60**, and Whisper large-v3 and Parakeet v3 tied at **4.81**. Those wins are real. They are also the wrong product. The five-language list omits Arabic, Hindi, Hebrew, Thai, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Ukrainian, and the live board’s dataset list had already changed by October 2026, so later snapshots are not the same measurement ([Srivastav et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961); [Voconly](https://www.voconly.com/en/blog/local-ai-asr/local-asr-model-comparison-2026/)).

Counted against Shroud’s thirty codes, the fast models fail first on coverage. Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 and Canary-1B-v2 share a 25-language European set. Eighteen of the thirty are in it, including Ukrainian and Croatian. The twelve that are not are Turkish, Arabic, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Norwegian Bokmål, Hebrew, Indonesian, Thai, Vietnamese, and Catalan ([Parakeet card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)). Inside the languages it does speak, NVIDIA’s own FLEURS table is uneven: Italian 3.00, English 4.85, and Ukrainian 6.79 sit next to Czech 11.01, Croatian 12.46, Finnish 13.21, Swedish 15.08, Hungarian 15.72, Danish 18.41, and Greek 20.70, for a 25-language average of **11.97%** that the five-language leaderboard never shows ([Parakeet card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)). Parakeet does auto-detect, and it emits punctuation plus word- and segment-level timestamps, which is the right control surface. It still needs at least 2 GB of RAM to load in NVIDIA’s own stack, its int8 ONNX encoder is about 623 MB before decoder and joiner, and it is not a Whisper file. whisper.cpp’s Parakeet path is a separate CLI merged in June 2026, not a flag on the Android Whisper API, and mobile and browser support for that ggml file was not established ([whisper.cpp parakeet-cli](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/blob/master/examples/parakeet-cli/README.md); [Parakeet card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)).

Qwen3-ASR, released 29 January 2026 under Apache-2.0, is the widest explicit list at a sub-2B size: 30 languages plus 22 Chinese dialects, with language identification in the same model. It hits 25 of Shroud’s codes and misses **Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian** ([Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)). On the vendor’s own FLEURS macro, the 1.7B beats Whisper large-v3 on a 12-language slice (**4.90 versus 5.27**) and loses once the slice widens to 30 languages (**12.60 versus 8.16**). The 0.6B, the size that could sit nearer a phone, falls to **21.80** on that same wide average. Language-ID accuracy on the vendor’s sets is a real win, **98.7% versus 94.6%** for the 1.7B on the 30-language FLEURS row, and the 0.6B is ahead of Whisper there too. Timestamps are a second checkpoint, Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B, limited to 11 languages. Official inference is Transformers or vLLM. Punctuation is not stated. No iOS or Android library is in the README ([Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR); [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1)).

Everything else in the 2025–2026 field fails the same gate more quickly. Voxtral Small 24B and Mini 3B are eight languages and the wrong size class; Voxtral 3, announced 22 October 2026, claims 50-plus languages and does not publish weights, and the printed figure still omits Hebrew and Croatian ([Mistral, July 2025](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral/); [Mistral, 22 Oct 2026](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3/)). Granite Speech 5.0 is six languages, English through Japanese. Phi-4-multimodal’s audio set is eight. Moonshine is a family of per-language models, not one detector, and its Korean and Ukrainian checkpoints stay under a non-commercial community licence. SenseVoice-Small’s released checkpoint is Mandarin, Cantonese, English, Japanese, and Korean. Kyutai is English, or English and French. Distil-Whisper, including large-v3.5 at 7.21% English WER, remains English-only, which is why the existing rejection of that line still holds ([Distil-Whisper](https://huggingface.co/distil-whisper); [Srivastav et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)). Gemma 3n is a multimodal model Google documents for on-device ASR, with a memory claim of about 2 GB for E2B, and the 35 multimodal languages were never listed. The Gemma Terms of Use require those use restrictions to be passed on to later users, which is not a permissive speech-model licence ([Gemma developer guide](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-gemma-3n-developer-guide/); [Gemma terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms)). Apple’s `SpeechTranscriber` on iOS 26 keeps audio on device after an Apple asset download, and Argmax’s June 2025 table records no language detection. The locale list is whatever the device reports, it is not Whisper’s set, and it does not exist on Android or the web ([SpeechTranscriber](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber); [Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax)).

sherpa-onnx is the only runtime found with production-shaped iOS, Android, and browser builds for models other than Whisper. The non-Whisper checkpoints it actually publishes, other than the Omnilingual CTC file below, are subsets: SenseVoice for five varieties, Canary-180M for four languages, Parakeet for the European 25, Moonshine per language ([sherpa-onnx](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/)). A split stack, Parakeet for Europe and Qwen or SenseVoice for the rest, still leaves Norwegian Bokmål, Hebrew, and Catalan without a model, and it would split punctuation and language-ID behavior across chats. That is the consistency problem a single Whisper checkpoint is already avoiding. ESPnet OWSM v4 is the other family whose training inventory covers the thirty codes, reconstructed from the v3.2 hour table plus v4’s own MLS Portuguese row, and the 370M model beats Whisper small on an eight-language MLS average (**11.8 versus 13.6 WER**) while remaining well behind Whisper large-v3 (**6.4**). The model card lists language identification and utterance-level timestamps. No ONNX, Core ML, ExecuTorch, GGML, or sherpa-onnx build was found, so OWSM stays in ESPnet ([OWSM v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00338); [small-model card](https://huggingface.co/espnet/owsm_v4_small_370M/blob/main/README.md)).

## Omnilingual CTC-300M passes five checks and fails the transcript

Omnilingual ASR is the model a coverage checklist correctly refuses to dismiss. Meta released it on 10 November 2025 under Apache-2.0. The language inventory is not the “1,600+” headline; it is `supported_langs` in the repository, read in full on 27 October 2026, and that file contains every code Shroud requires, with the expected script tags: `ukr_Cyrl`, `nob_Latn` for Bokmål, `cat_Latn`, `hrv_Latn`, `heb_Hebr`, `tha_Thai`, `arb_Arab`, `hin_Deva`, `jpn_Jpan`, `kor_Hang`, and Mandarin as `cmn_Hans` and `cmn_Hant` rather than a bare `zh` ([lang_ids.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/main/src/omnilingual_asr/models/wav2vec2_llama/lang_ids.py); [model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-300M/raw/main/README.md)). The phone-shaped checkpoint is the CTC 300M, **325 million parameters and 1.3 GiB in FP32**, not the model card’s “LLM-300M,” which is **1.63 billion parameters and 6.1 GiB** because a 1.2B decoder sits on the 300M encoder. sherpa-onnx ships the CTC int8 ONNX at **348 MiB**, and a November 2025 Android report loads that exact file. The official speed row is an A100 figure, RTF 0.001 at about 2 GiB for unquantized CTC, and no iPhone or Android real-time factor was published for the int8 file ([model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-300M/raw/main/README.md); [sherpa models](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/omnilingual-asr/models.html); [sherpa-onnx issue 2812](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/2812)).

The quality comparison is also real, and it is easy to over-read. On the November 2025 paper’s average character error rate, restricted to languages Whisper covers, CTC 300M scores **11.8 on FLEURS-81, 4.1 on MLS-8, and 17.6 on Common Voice**, against Whisper small at **50.8, 4.9, and 111.7** and Whisper large-v3 at **22.6, 2.0, and 55.6**. The authors say the 300M CTC beats Whisper small and medium on all four benchmarks in that table and falls behind Whisper large-v3 only on MLS-8. The FLEURS average of 22.6 for large-v3 is not an English error rate. It is pulled up by languages outside the high-resource set, there is no per-language table for Ukrainian, Hebrew, Thai, Hindi, or Chinese, the metric is CER rather than WER, and the December 2025 v2 checkpoints have no Whisper table at all ([Omnilingual paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09690)). The 7B LLM-ASR, which does better still (FLEURS-81 CER 5.6), is a different object: on the March 2026 English leaderboard the 7B v2 is **worse** than Whisper large-v3 (**8.14 versus 7.44**) at RTFx 66 versus 146, and its five-language European win (**4.39 versus 4.81**) arrives at RTFx 21 ([Srivastav et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)). None of those rows is an on-device int8 measurement.

The blocking gaps are the ones the survival checklist left out on purpose. Meta’s inference README states that the models “are trained to output transcripts in spoken form without any punctuation or capitalization,” and it points at a third-party punctuation library while noting that most such libraries cover only a small subset of the 1,600 languages. CTC v2 “don’t support language conditioning.” The LLM variants can be given a language code; that is a hint, not a reported language-ID accuracy, and omitting the code is not documented as detection. No page describes word, token, or utterance timestamps. The Android sherpa config that actually loads on a phone exposes the model file and not a language ([inference README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/blob/main/src/omnilingual_asr/models/inference/README.md); [issue 2812](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/2812)). Shroud detects the language once per note, stores those probabilities as a per-chat history, and takes punctuation from the same decode. CTC-300M can do none of those three. A second punctuation model would reintroduce the pass the product already refused when it kept Whisper, and it would not cover the language set. The LLM checkpoints that accept a language code are still spoken-form text, at about 5 GiB of the authors’ own VRAM row for the smallest of them. “Survives” means the five mechanical checks were met. It does not mean this is a better voice-note engine.

## Measured silence and speed wins belong to other products

Replacing Whisper to escape hallucinations is not supported. On 42 pure room-tone clips, a September 2026 paper found large-v3 emitting words on **61.9%** and turbo on **59.5%**, and both emitting something on every clip. On 1,168 non-speech clips the word rates were **89.9%** and **80.7%**. Turbo is not a fix. The fine-tune in that paper that drove silence emissions down also deleted real speech, and it was not shown on the thirty languages ([Zolkepli](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32560v2)). An earlier non-speech study found Whisper large-v3 at **99.97%** hallucination on UrbanSound8K when outputs were counted before the no-speech filter, against **13.52%** for a Conformer-CTC baseline. A different protocol that keeps `no_speech_threshold` at 0.6 reports the same large-v3 model at 76%, so the rate is a methodology choice as much as a model property ([Calm-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12969v1); [Scicom dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Scicom-intl/Whisper-Hallucination)). No retrieved study measures Qwen3-ASR, Parakeet v3, Canary-1B-v2, Moonshine, or Omnilingual on that non-speech set. A CTC or TDT decoder cannot loop an autoregressive text decoder the way Whisper can, and the Conformer-CTC result is evidence that the class is quieter on one English corpus. It is not a measurement of a model that covers this language list, and it is not a reason to drop punctuation and language ID to obtain it. Whisper’s `no_speech_threshold`, log-probability threshold, and compression-ratio threshold are already the production controls for a failure mode the papers document and the replacements do not ([turbo card](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)).

The speed story has the same shape. Parakeet v3’s leaderboard RTFx, on the order of twenty times Whisper large-v3 on an A100 at batch 64, is an architectural fact about TDT versus a transformer decoder. It is not a phone budget. One vendor measured Parakeet v3 at about 49 seconds for an hour of audio on a Mac Neural Engine, roughly 73× realtime, and said so for that Mac, not for an iPhone ([Whisper Notes](https://whispernotes.app/zh/blog/parakeet-ultra-vs-parakeet-v3)). Qwen3-ASR-1.7B’s English WER advantage arrives at essentially Whisper large-v3’s RTFx (**148 versus 146**), and the 0.6B’s “2000 times throughput” line is a batched server claim ([Srivastav et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961); [Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)). Whisper’s fixed 30-second encoder window does make a short note pay for a full window, which is why a variable-length model can look dramatically faster on a tiny clip. The only phone number in hand is one third-party iPhone 14 Pro Max anecdote, Whisper small at 45.9 seconds for a 3.3-second question versus a fraction of a second for a Moonshine graph. One clip is not a migration plan, and Moonshine does not identify language across the thirty codes ([moonshine-tiny-en-onnx](https://huggingface.co/vasyadeva/moonshine-tiny-en-onnx)). The binding phone constraint remains weight file plus peak RAM. A Redmi Note 11 with 4 GB of RAM loaded SenseVoice int8, a 239 MB file, at an app peak around **700–770 MB**. Parakeet’s int8 pack is already about 640 MB of weights before ONNX Runtime’s overhead, and NVIDIA’s card asks for 2 GB to load ([sherpa-onnx issue 4056](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/4056); [Parakeet card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)).

## The gain versus small is still a Whisper checkpoint

Within Whisper, the step that matches the accuracy problem is a larger checkpoint, not a new family. The only direct small-versus-turbo-versus-large-v3 sample retrieved is a July 2026 FLEURS run, 50 utterances per language. large-v3 / turbo / small: English **4.1 / 4.7 / 6.6**, French **5.8 / 6.7 / 14.3**, German **4.6 / 6.0 / 12.8**, Mandarin CER **6.7 / 8.0 / 19.5**, Egyptian Arabic **14.6 / 16.1 / 32.7**, Hindi **15.7 / 22.3 / 42.4**. Turbo takes most of large-v3’s gain over small on the six European languages in that sample, about 0.8 points behind large-v3, and on Arabic, Japanese, and Korean. It gives back a large share on Hindi, 6.6 points behind large-v3, and the sample does not include Thai ([Vocova](https://vocova.app/blog/ai-transcription-accuracy-benchmark-2026)). OpenAI’s own October 2024 note is the Thai warning: turbo keeps the large-v3 encoder, cuts the decoder from 32 layers to 4, and is a fine-tune on large-v3’s multilingual transcription data, not a Distil-Whisper student. Across languages the maintainer describes it as similar to large-v2, with larger degradation on Thai and Cantonese, and translation was left out of training ([OpenAI discussion](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)). On the March 2026 English macro the large-v3 to turbo gap is **0.39 WER (7.44 to 7.83)** at RTFx 146 versus 200. Long-form English actually has turbo slightly ahead, **11.0 versus 11.2**. The often-quoted “about 8×” speedup versus large-v3 is a different regime from that batch-64 result, which compresses the decoder-depth advantage to about 1.4×. Single-stream phone latency for turbo was not measured ([Srivastav et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)).

The files that fit a phone default already exist inside whisper.cpp: multilingual **small at 466 MiB**, **large-v3 at 2.9 GiB**, **large-v3 q5_0 at 1.1 GiB**, **large-v3-turbo at 1.5 GiB**, and **large-v3-turbo q5_0 at 547 MiB**. Full large-v3 is a poor mid-range default. q5_0 large-v3 is the size compromise that keeps the 32-layer decoder; quantization error against fp16 was not measured, so it is not yet known to be no worse on every language. Turbo q5_0 is the download and speed option, and it is not allowed to ship on the evidence above if Thai, Hindi, or any other required language is worse than the checkpoint it replaces. WhisperKit’s open-source README already recommends a compressed multilingual large-v3 turbo at **626 MB**. This pass did not confirm that the current WhisperKit pin or the transformers.js web build loads the same turbo checkpoint, and it did not confirm that the Android pin, whisper.cpp 1.9.4, loads turbo even though current master publishes the file ([whisper.cpp models](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master/models); [WhisperKit](https://github.com/argmaxinc/argmax-oss-swift/blob/main/README.md)). The research baseline of “about Whisper small on iOS and web, quantized small or base on Android” was the assignment’s default and was not re-read from the app. Against that baseline, moving up inside Whisper is an upgrade. Against an already-shipped large-v3, turbo is a speed trade with a named Thai regression, not a free accuracy win. Silence behavior does not decide it: turbo is about as willing as large-v3 to emit text on room tone ([Zolkepli](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32560v2)).

Leaving the family would also drop the shared surface the three clients already call. Whisper predicts the language in the same model that decodes, returns segment and word timestamps, accepts a text prompt, and exposes the silence thresholds above ([turbo card](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)). Parakeet has detection, punctuation, and timestamps inside 25 languages and does not document a language-probability vector or a contact-name prompt. Qwen returns a language string on its own list and needs a second model for timestamps. Omnilingual CTC returns neither a language nor punctuation. One Whisper checkpoint is what ggml, Core ML, and ONNX already know how to run. A new architecture is three new exports, or one new runtime on three operating systems, and then a thirty-language parity test the new model cannot pass because the languages are not in it. Offline utilities that publish a model list in 2026 already behave this way: Anti-Vocale ships Parakeet for the 25 European languages and Whisper Turbo when it needs the long tail ([F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/packages/com.antivocale.app/)).

## Conclusion

The 2026 leaderboard is scored for a different product than a multilingual messenger: English or five western European languages, punctuation removed before the metric, and a datacenter GPU at batch 64. Models that win that contest did it by narrowing the language set or by growing into a 7B–24B network. Shroud’s constraints, thirty languages, one language decision with a probability the chat can remember, punctuation from the same weights, and a file a phone can download, are the constraints Whisper still matches and the specialist models traded away. The word “survives” on Omnilingual CTC-300M is a category error if it is read as a verdict. It means the weights, the licence, the code list, a flattering average CER, and a third-party int8 runtime all exist. The transcript the app shows the user would get worse in the ways the user can see, even where the character error rate got better.

The engineering work that follows is an in-family checkpoint trial, not a sherpa-onnx migration. Prefer quantized large-v3 where about 1.1 GiB and the unpruned decoder are acceptable, and treat turbo, including the 547 MiB q5_0 file and WhisperKit’s 626 MB recommendation, as the speed option that has to clear Thai and Hindi against today’s notes before it becomes the default. Reopen a switch only if a later checkpoint adds the five languages Qwen still lacks, or adds punctuation and a real language probability to a phone-sized Omnilingual model, and ships maintained runtimes on all three clients. Neither checkpoint was in the record on 27 October 2026.
