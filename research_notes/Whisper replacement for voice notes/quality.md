# Open ASR quality and speed versus Whisper for 30-language on-device voice notes

Scope is published accuracy, language identification, non-speech hallucination, and speed-versus-size evidence as of 2026-10-27. The product language set is en, de, es, fr, it, pt, nl, pl, ru, uk, tr, ar, hi, ja, ko, zh, sv, da, nb, fi, cs, el, he, id, th, vi, ro, hu, ca, hr. Current defaults called out by the task are about Whisper small (iOS and web) and a quantized Whisper small or base (Android). This note does not recommend a model.

Two evaluation regimes must not be mixed. The Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard reports macro-average word error rate after punctuation, casing, and English-style text normalization are stripped, plus inverse real-time factor (RTFx = audio duration / transcription time, higher is faster) on a datacenter GPU with a large batch. Model-card FLEURS or Common Voice numbers are often a different text normalization, sometimes a subset of languages, and sometimes the vendor’s own harness with language left unset. A lower number on one table is not a win on the other.

## What do public boards show in 2025–2026 for Whisper and the named open models?

### Takeaway

On the March 2026 Open ASR Leaderboard paper, several open models beat Whisper large-v3 on English and on a five-language European subset, but the models that clearly beat it are either English-only, limited to about 4–25 European languages, or multi-billion-parameter. No retrieved 2025–2026 public table shows one open model beating Whisper large-v3 on the full 30-language set at a phone-sized footprint. Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is the closest broad open model, and its own FLEURS average over 30 languages is worse than Whisper large-v3 even though it wins the 12-language subset and the English leaderboard.

### Cited Findings

#### How to read the Open ASR Leaderboard

- The benchmark paper is Srivastav et al., arXiv:2510.06961v4, dated 30 March 2026. Counts in the paper are as of 27 March 2026: 86 systems, 74 open, 12 datasets. The live table is at the Hugging Face space, and the paper says that table keeps moving. — [ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961); [space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard)
- Short-form English (“Leaderboard”) is audio under 30 seconds on AMI, Earnings22 (a 5-hour subset), GigaSpeech, LibriSpeech clean, LibriSpeech other, SPGISpeech, TED-LIUM v3, and VoxPopuli. LibriSpeech and Earnings22 are English-only and are not multilingual evidence. The whole short-form track is English-only. — [ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- The paper’s multilingual track is only German, French, Italian, Spanish, and Portuguese, using CoVoST-2, FLEURS, and MLS. It is not a 30-language or FLEURS-102 result. — [ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- Long-form is English and longer than 30 seconds (Earnings21, Earnings22, TED-LIUM v3, CORAAL). — [ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- WER is computed after normalization that removes punctuation and casing. The leaderboard therefore does not score punctuation. RTFx in the paper was measured on one NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB, batch size 64 when it fit. The paper states that absolute RTFx does not transfer to other hardware. — [ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- By 17 October 2026 the space’s loader listed a different English set, including cleaned sets plus URGENT24 and private sets, and separate files for French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Hindi, Dutch, and Armenian. Averages from that code are not the March 2026 averages. — [space init.py mirror, versions through 17-10-2026](https://hugging-face.cn/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard/raw/main/init.py)
- A 19 October 2026 write-up says absolute Open ASR means moved because test sets and normalization changed, and that a second-half-2026 results dataset and a September 2026 snapshot cannot be lined up with the March launch numbers. — [Voconly, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.voconly.com/en/blog/local-ai-asr/local-asr-model-comparison-2026/)

#### Whisper large-v3, turbo, small, and distil

- large-v3-turbo was announced 1 October 2024 by OpenAI: the large-v3 encoder kept, decoder cut from 32 layers to 4, then fine-tuned for two epochs on the large-v3 multilingual transcription data. It is not a Distil-Whisper student. Translation was left out. OpenAI’s maintainer wrote that across languages it performs similarly to large-v2, with larger degradation on Thai and Cantonese, and that it looks better on FLEURS than on Common Voice. Italicized languages in their figure use CER; Korean was switched to CER because spacing makes WER harsh. The announcement points at a figure for the per-language plot; a full numeric table was not in the text retrieved here. — [OpenAI Whisper discussion #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)
- Sizes, from a 2026 comparison that attributes parameter counts and relative speed to the OpenAI Whisper README and file sizes to faster-whisper CT2 builds: tiny 39M, base 74M, small 244M (~486 MB, about 4× large-v3), medium 769M, large-v3-turbo 809M (~1.62 GB, about 8× large-v3), large-v3 1550M (~3.09 GB, 1×). There is no English-only turbo or large. — [Sablate, 11 Sep 2026](https://sablate.com/blog/whisper-model-comparison-subtitles)
- That “about 8×” speed claim conflicts with the March 2026 leaderboard, where turbo’s RTFx is 200 versus large-v3’s 146 (about 1.4×) under A100 batch-64. Both figures are cited; they are different regimes. Single-stream decoder pruning shows up more when the batch is 1. Batched GPU RTFx compresses it. — [Sablate, 11 Sep 2026](https://sablate.com/blog/whisper-model-comparison-subtitles); [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- A July 2026 FLEURS sample, 50 utterances per language, seed-fixed, three Whisper checkpoints, is the only retrieved head-to-head of small vs turbo vs large-v3 on several product languages. WER unless marked CER. large-v3 / turbo / small: English 4.1 / 4.7 / 6.6; Spanish 2.9 / 3.3 / 6.5; French 5.8 / 6.7 / 14.3; German 4.6 / 6.0 / 12.8; Italian 3.2 / 3.9 / 8.2; Portuguese 6.6 / 7.2 / 11.0; Japanese CER 6.6 / 5.8 / 16.4; Korean 14.9 / 14.5 / 21.7; Mandarin CER 6.7 / 8.0 / 19.5; Cantonese CER 10.5 / 43.3 / not supported; Hindi 15.7 / 22.3 / 42.4; Arabic (Egypt) 14.6 / 16.1 / 32.7. The authors say turbo trails large-v3 by about 0.8 points on the six European languages in this sample, and by 6.6 points on Hindi. Fifty utterances is a small sample. Cantonese is not in the product list; Thai is, and this sample does not include Thai. — [Vocova, 18 Jul 2026](https://vocova.app/blog/ai-transcription-accuracy-benchmark-2026)
- Distil-Whisper large-v3.5 on the March 2026 English track: 7.21 average WER and RTFx 202, versus Whisper large-v3 at 7.44 and 146. The language column is 1. It is an English model, not a multilingual replacement. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)

## Do successors fix Whisper’s silence and noise hallucinations, and where is that measured?

### Takeaway

Whisper large-v3 and turbo still emit text on a large share of non-speech under 2025–2026 measurement papers. A Conformer-CTC baseline in one of those papers is far quieter. Fine-tunes that add non-speech can cut Whisper’s silence emissions, and the checkpoint that is quietest also deletes real speech. No retrieved study shows Qwen3-ASR, Parakeet v3, Canary-1B-v2, Moonshine, or SenseVoice measured on the same non-speech set as Whisper. One multi-model hallucination count even flags Parakeet and Canary more often than Whisper large-v3, under a protocol that is not the silence test.

### Cited Findings

- Calm-Whisper, arXiv:2505.12969, 19 May 2025, UrbanSound8K non-speech: Whisper large-v3 hallucination rate 99.97%, a Conformer-CTC-large baseline 13.52%. Three of 20 decoder self-attention heads accounted for over 75% of the hallucinations. Fine-tuning those three heads for five epochs: hallucination rate 15.51%, LibriSpeech test-clean WER 2.19 versus 2.12 unmasked, test-other 4.13 versus 4.07. The abstract’s “over 80% reduction” and “less than 0.1% WER degradation” match that table in direction; the clean-set move is +0.07 absolute WER. Masking the three heads without fine-tuning dropped hallucinations to 24.10% but raised clean WER to 3.57 and other to 5.98. — [Calm-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12969v1)
- A dataset card that compares published rates warns that the same large-v3 model is reported at 99.97% on UrbanSound8K by Calm-Whisper and at 76.08% by another paper, because the latter keeps Whisper’s no-speech filter at `no_speech_threshold = 0.6` and the former counts model output before that filter. Rates more than 10× apart are a methodology gap, not a model change. — [Scicom Whisper-Hallucination dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Scicom-intl/Whisper-Hallucination)
- Barański et al., ICASSP 2025, arXiv:2501.11378: on non-speech audio, 121,378 of 301,317 Whisper inferences (40.3%) produced a transcript containing letters or numbers after symbol-only outputs were discarded; 9.1% of those hallucinations looped. Hallucination rate rose once files exceeded 30 seconds, which is Whisper’s chunk length. A separate augmentation table lists 17.1% detected hallucinations when speech was padded with silence and 21.1% when padded with non-speech. This protocol is not the UrbanSound rate. — [Barański et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378v1)
- Zolkepli, arXiv:2609.32560, 26 September 2026 (HTML updated 20 October 2026). On 42 pure room-tone clips, share emitting words: large-v2 85.7%, large-v3 61.9%, turbo 59.5%. Share emitting anything on those 42 clips: large-v3 100%, turbo 100%. Music, 600 clips, words emitted: large-v3 97.0%, turbo 96.7%. Non-speech, 1,168 clips: large-v3 89.9%, turbo 80.7%. Turbo is not a fix. A Malaysian-turbo-v3 fine-tune that added non-speech negatives reached 16.7% on silence and 9.3% on non-speech, and the paper says that checkpoint recovered only 41.1% of genuinely spoken phrases versus 69.8% for the base, deleted 58.0% of repeated-speech clips, and had character error rate 1.26 on a typical FLEURS language. A different fine-tune that also trains on synthetic positives of the hallucinated phrases is reported to move silence word-emission from 61.9% to 2.4% and phrase recovery from 69.8% to 82.7%, while cutting words on real voice-free audio only from 99.9% to 47.8%. These are Whisper fine-tunes, not a new architecture, and they are not shown on the 30 product languages. — [How to Reduce Whisper Hallucination](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32560v2)
- The same dataset card’s HALAS flagged counts out of 3,611: whisper-large-v2 1,581, parakeet-tdt-v2 1,217, canary-1b 1,213, canary-1b-flash 1,099, phi-4 1,096, whisper-large-v3-turbo 1,060, granite 1,002, whisper-large-v3 858, CrisperWhisper 772. On this count, turbo is worse than large-v3, and Parakeet v2 and Canary-1B are worse than large-v3. The card does not, in the portion retrieved, define HALAS as a pure silence test, and it does not include Parakeet v3, Qwen3-ASR, Moonshine, or SenseVoice. — [Scicom Whisper-Hallucination dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Scicom-intl/Whisper-Hallucination)
- SenseVoice’s generate API accepts `language="nospeech"` alongside zn, en, yue, ja, ko. That shows a non-speech label exists. No non-speech emission rate was published next to it. — [SenseVoiceSmall model card](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/SenseVoiceSmall)
- CTC and TDT decoders cannot freely loop an autoregressive text decoder the way Whisper can. Calm-Whisper’s Conformer-CTC result (13.52% vs 99.97% before the no-speech filter) is the concrete evidence that this class is quieter on that set. It is one English non-speech corpus, not a 30-language voice-note test, and it is not a measurement of Parakeet v3 or Canary-1B-v2. — [Calm-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12969v1)

### Inferences

- “Newer open ASR” is not, on the evidence retrieved, a demonstrated fix for silence hallucinations. The measured fixes are either a different decoder family on one dataset (Conformer-CTC) or Whisper fine-tunes that trade missed speech for silence.
- Turbo does not materially fix the failure. On the 2026 silence/music/non-speech arms it is close to large-v3.
- For short voice notes, the production-relevant failure is text on silence or noise inside a clip the user meant to send. The papers measure that for Whisper. They do not measure it for the models that beat Whisper on English WER.

### Gaps

- No non-speech or silence emission rate was found for Qwen3-ASR-0.6B/1.7B, Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3, Canary-1B-v2, Moonshine, Kyutai, Granite, Voxtral, Omnilingual, or SenseVoice on a shared corpus.
- The HALAS counts are not a silence benchmark and do not cover the v3 Parakeet/Canary models. They should not be read as “Parakeet hallucinates more” without the protocol, only as a published count that does not show those models as cleaner than Whisper large-v3.
- Repetition/loop rate for short clips (voice notes are often under 30 seconds) is thinner than the long-file result in Barański et al. The 2026 silence arm is only 42 clips.

## How does language identification compare with Whisper on short audio?

### Takeaway

The one primary head-to-head retrieved is Qwen’s own: on their sets, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B and even the 0.6B beat Whisper large-v3 at language identification, with the gap largest on Common Voice and on conversational MLC-SLM, and essentially tied on MLS. That evaluation’s broadest set is Qwen’s 30 languages, which omits Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian. Parakeet and Canary advertise detection inside 25 European languages without a published accuracy. A small external spot check shows Whisper large-v3 auto-detect collapsing on Cantonese versus Thai.

### Cited Findings

- MLS in that table is eight high-resource European languages (da, de, en, es, fr, it, pl, pt), where Whisper is already at 99.9. The gains are on Common Voice (includes zh, yue, ar, ja, ko) and MLC-SLM (includes ja, ko, th, vi). — [Qwen3-ASR README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)
- Parakeet v3 and a GGUF packaging of it are described as auto-detecting among the 25 European languages with no language prefix. ### Inferences

- On the language sets Qwen measured, replacing Whisper’s language id with Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is a published gain, including at the 0.6B size, and the gain is larger where Whisper is already imperfect (Common Voice, MLC-SLM) than on clean European audiobooks.
- That does not cover short-clip confusion among the product languages Qwen does not train (uk vs ru, he, nb vs da, ca vs es, hr vs other South Slavic). Those pairs are unmeasured here.
- Parakeet/Canary auto-detect cannot be compared with Whisper’s 99-way id. It is a 25-way European decision, and the accuracy of that decision was not found.

### Gaps

- No LID accuracy for Parakeet v3, Canary-1B-v2, SenseVoice, Moonshine, Kyutai, Granite, or Omnilingual versus Whisper.
- No measurement of identification from a short prefix (the first second or two of a voice note) for any model, including Whisper.
- No confusion matrix for Whisper or Qwen on Ukrainian/Russian, Catalan/Spanish, Norwegian/Danish, or Hebrew.

## What is known about speed versus size, and which wins are datacenter-only?

### Takeaway

Leaderboard RTFx is an A100, often batch-64, number. It is useful for ranking architectures and misleading as a phone budget. On that machine, 600M TDT Parakeet is on the order of 20× Whisper large-v3, while Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is about the same speed as Whisper large-v3 and Voxtral 24B and Omnilingual 7B are slower. Phone-plausible sizes in the evidence are Whisper small at 244M, SenseVoice-Small at about 234M, Moonshine from 26M to 245M per language, Parakeet/Canary-class models around 0.6B–1B, and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B. The 0.6B Qwen and the European 0.6B Parakeet are the only near-phone models with both a size and a published accuracy story, and they fail different parts of the language set. No iPhone or Android real-time factor was retrieved for Qwen, Parakeet, or Canary.

### Cited Findings

- RTFx definition and hardware: audio duration divided by transcription time, one A100 80GB, batch 64 reduced only when memory required it. Absolute values “may not directly transfer.” — [ASR Leaderboard paper, §2.3 and §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- March 2026 English RTFx next to size, open models: Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 3390 (0.6B, English), Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 3330 (0.6B, 25 languages), Parakeet CTC 1.1B 2730 (English), Canary-1B-v2 749 (about 1B, 25 languages), Cohere Transcribe 525, Canary-Qwen 2.5B 418, Granite Speech 4.0 1B 280, Distil-Whisper large-v3.5 202 (English), Whisper large-v3-turbo 200 (809M), Qwen3-ASR 1.7B 148, Whisper large-v3 146 (1.55B), Granite 3.3 8B 145, Voxtral Small 24B 54.1, Omnilingual 7B v2 66. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961); [Sablate, 11 Sep 2026](https://sablate.com/blog/whisper-model-comparison-subtitles)
- Same paper, multilingual RTFx on the five-language track: Parakeet v3 1720, Canary-1B-v2 634, Cohere 491, Qwen3-ASR 1.7B 113, Whisper large-v3 111, Phi-4 78.2, Voxtral Small 24B 42.0, Omnilingual 7B v2 21.2. Parakeet’s speed advantage survives the multilingual harness. Qwen 1.7B does not pull ahead of Whisper large-v3. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- Long-form English RTFx: Parakeet v3 1000, turbo 148, large-v3 68.6, Canary-Qwen 2.5B 16.1. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- The 9 October 2026 live-board reading is the same shape at different scale: Parakeet v3 English RTFx 6076, turbo 795, large-v3 470; six-language RTFx 3372 / 599 / 346. Still a datacenter board. — [Yaps, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.yaps.ai/blog/parakeet-vs-whisper)
- Whisper file sizes aimed at local builds: small about 244M and 486 MB, turbo about 809M and 1.62 GB in fp16, large-v3 about 1.55B and 3.09 GB. A Q8_0 turbo build is listed at 0.83 GB. On an AMD 4750U, that Q8 turbo build is described as 3.1× realtime on the GPU and 1.4× realtime on the CPU. That is a laptop APU, not a phone, and it is one implementation. — [Sablate, 11 Sep 2026](https://sablate.com/blog/whisper-model-comparison-subtitles); [handy.computer turbo card](https://models.handy.computer/models/whisper-large-v3-turbo/)
- Parakeet v3 GGUF sizes from a third-party conversion: fp16 1.26 GB, Q8_0 711 MB, Q5_0 516 MB, Q4_K 467 MB. The card also repeats a 6.34% Open ASR average that does not match either the March paper’s 6.32% or the October Yaps reading of 5.66%. Size figures are the useful part; the WER on that card should not be preferred over the paper. — [Parakeet v3 GGUF card](https://huggingface.co/cstr/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-GGUF)
- An app vendor measured Parakeet v3 and Parakeet Ultra on a Mac, Apple Neural Engine, Core ML conversion, at about 49 seconds to process one hour of audio for both (about 73× realtime on that Mac). Not a phone. — [Whisper Notes, 27 Oct 2026](https://whispernotes.app/zh/blog/parakeet-ultra-vs-parakeet-v3)
- A Mac-only Parakeet-versus-Whisper article (18 October 2026) says Parakeet was about 6× faster than Whisper on their Apple Silicon tests and explicitly tells readers not to use leaderboard RTFx as the speed number for a Mac. It does not give an iPhone figure. — [TranscribeX, 18 Oct 2026](https://www.transcribex.io/blog/parakeet-vs-whisper/)
- SenseVoice-Small, about 234M: vendor latency “70 ms to process 10 seconds,” which is about 143× realtime if taken as a single 10-second clip, plus the conflicting 5×/7× versus Whisper-Small and 15×/17× versus Whisper-Large claims noted above. An unofficial fp16 pack is listed at 448 MB and a q8 pack at 241 MB, with a claimed 5.6× realtime on that packager’s runtime. — [SenseVoiceSmall model card](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/SenseVoiceSmall); [OpenASR model page](https://openasr.org/models/sensevoice-small/)
- Moonshine’s on-device pitch is the size column itself: 26M–245M English, 34M–123M for the streaming non-English checkpoints. The docs do not give RTFx on a phone. — [Moonshine model list](https://moonshine-voice.readthedocs.io/en/stable/models/available-models/)
- Qwen3-ASR-0.6B “2000 times throughput at concurrency 128” is a batched GPU claim. The leaderboard did not print an RTFx for the 0.6B checkpoint in the March subset. The 1.7B’s leaderboard RTFx (148 English, 113 on five languages) matches Whisper large-v3, not Parakeet. — [Qwen3-ASR README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR); [ASR Leaderboard paper, Tables 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- Voxtral Small at 24B and RTFx 42–54, Omnilingual 7B at RTFx 21–66, Granite 8B at RTFx 145, and Kyutai at 1B–2.6B with a 0.5–2.5 second streaming delay are accuracy or latency results from accelerators, not evidence that the model fits beside a phone app. Kyutai’s own page says batching supports hundreds of concurrent streams on one GPU. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Tables 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961); [Kyutai STT page](https://kyutai.org/stt)
- Coval’s hosted latency board, last run 27 October 2026, ranks API deployments (Qwen3-ASR 1.7B time-to-final-segment 37 ms on Baseten, Parakeet v3 96 ms on Together, Whisper large-v3 126 ms on Baseten). That is network serving latency, not on-device RTFx, and the page’s WER column is a different evaluation from FLEURS or Open ASR. It is not used here as accuracy evidence. — [Coval Whisper large-v3](https://benchmarks.coval.ai/models/whisper-large-v3)

### Inferences

- A model that needs a data-center GPU to show its WER advantage is not, on this evidence, a win for on-device voice notes. Voxtral 24B, Omnilingual 7B, and Granite 8B fall in that bucket on size even when their European WER is better.
- Qwen3-ASR-1.7B’s English WER advantage over Whisper large-v3 on the March board (5.76 vs 7.44) arrives at essentially the same A100 RTFx. It is not a speed upgrade over large-v3, and it is a much larger weight than Whisper small (244M). The 0.6B Qwen is the size that could sit nearer a phone, and it is the checkpoint that loses badly on the vendor’s 30-language FLEURS average.
- Parakeet 0.6B is the rare case of a large measured speedup at a size that quantizes under about 0.5–0.7 GB. The speedup is real on the published GPU numbers and on one vendor’s Mac measurement. The language coverage is the constraint, not the speed.
- Leaderboard RTFx overstates CTC/TDT relative to a single short voice note, because batch 64 is how those decoders pull away, and it understates turbo relative to large-v3, because decoder depth matters more at batch 1. Single-stream phone measurements are still missing.

### Gaps

- No iPhone, Android, or Core ML/NNAPI real-time factor was found for Qwen3-ASR, Canary-1B-v2, Granite 1B, or SenseVoice on-device beyond vendor claims and laptop/Mac reports.
- Memory high-water during int8 inference on a phone was not found for any model except packagers’ file sizes.
- Qwen3-ASR-0.6B has no Open ASR RTFx in the March printed table.
- Whisper small’s RTFx on the Open ASR hardware was not in the printed subset, so small-versus-Parakeet speed on equal hardware is not known. Only the parameter ratio (244M vs 600M) and OpenAI’s relative-speed notes are.

## Does Whisper large-v3-turbo already capture most of the gain versus small?

### Takeaway

On English leaderboards, turbo sits close to large-v3 (a few tenths of a point on the March 2026 average, somewhat more on later snapshots) and well ahead of where small is usually understood to sit, but small itself is not on that printed leaderboard. The only direct small-versus-turbo-versus-large-v3 numbers retrieved are a 50-utterance-per-language FLEURS sample: turbo captures most of the large-v3 gain on six European languages and on Arabic, Japanese, and Korean in that sample, and it does not capture it on Hindi or Cantonese. OpenAI’s own note says Thai and Cantonese degrade more. Thai is a product language. Staying inside Whisper with a larger checkpoint is therefore supported as an accuracy move over small for high-resource languages, and not supported as equivalent to large-v3 for every product language.

### Cited Findings

- March 2026 English macro, same harness: large-v3 7.44%, turbo 7.83% (+0.39), RTFx 146 versus 200. Long-form English: large-v3 11.2%, turbo 11.0%. Turbo is not uniformly worse once the audio is long. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Tables 3 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- OpenAI, 1 October 2024: turbo matches large-v2 more than it matches a claim of equaling large-v3 on every language, and “shows larger degradation on some languages like Thai and Cantonese.” Decoder is 4 layers, same as tiny, encoder unchanged. No translation task. — [OpenAI Whisper discussion #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)
- Parameter and nominal speed step from small to turbo, as cited from the OpenAI README by a 2026 comparison: 244M and about 4× large-v3, versus 809M and about 8× large-v3. Turbo is larger than small, not a shrink of small, and the nominal speed is still higher than large-v3 because the decoder is tiny. The A100 batch-64 result compresses that speedup to about 1.4×. — [Sablate, 11 Sep 2026](https://sablate.com/blog/whisper-model-comparison-subtitles); [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- July 2026, 50 FLEURS utterances per language. Absolute WER or CER, large-v3 minus turbo, and small minus large-v3, from the published table: English +0.6 turbo and +2.5 small; Spanish +0.4 and +3.6; French +0.9 and +8.5; German +1.4 and +8.2; Italian +0.7 and +5.0; Portuguese +0.6 and +4.4; Japanese CER turbo better by 0.8, small worse by 9.8; Korean turbo better by 0.4, small worse by 6.8; Mandarin CER +1.3 and +12.8; Hindi +6.6 and +26.7; Egyptian Arabic +1.5 and +18.1; Cantonese CER +32.8 and unsupported on small. The authors’ summary: about 0.8 points on six European languages, 6.6 points on Hindi. — [Vocova, 18 Jul 2026](https://vocova.app/blog/ai-transcription-accuracy-benchmark-2026)
- October 2026 six-European-language leaderboard reading, different harness from the July sample: large-v3 4.57%, turbo 5.41% (+0.84), Parakeet v3 5.25%. Turbo and Parakeet are close to each other on that European slice, and both trail large-v3. — [Yaps, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.yaps.ai/blog/parakeet-vs-whisper)
- August 2026 app-vendor FLEURS run of turbo versus Qwen, not versus small, still shows turbo weak where OpenAI warned and where the July sample was weak: Cantonese 36.75, Hindi 29.64, Thai 13.40, against Qwen 1.7B at 6.44, 13.19, and 6.53. Those turbo figures are harsher than the July 50-utterance Hindi 22.3 and are a different sample. Both say turbo is not large-v3 and is not Qwen on Hindi and Thai. — [Whisper Notes, 25 Aug 2026](https://whispernotes.app/blog/qwen3-asr-vs-whisper); [Vocova, 18 Jul 2026](https://vocova.app/blog/ai-transcription-accuracy-benchmark-2026)
- Distil-Whisper large-v3.5 improves the English average to 7.21 from large-v3’s 7.44 and is English-only, so it does not carry the multilingual half of “most of the gain versus small.” — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)
- Whisper small is absent from the March 2026 printed leaderboard subset, so there is no official Open ASR number for the model the product uses as the default. — [ASR Leaderboard paper, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961)

### Inferences

- On high-resource European languages, published point gaps from large-v3 to turbo are under about one absolute WER point on both the March English macro (+0.39) and the small FLEURS sample (about +0.8 on six languages). The gap from small to large-v3 in that sample is several points even in Europe and tens of points for Hindi, Arabic, and Mandarin. Most of the within-Whisper accuracy gain versus small, on those languages, is already in turbo.
- That statement is not safe for Thai, Cantonese, or Hindi. OpenAI named Thai and Cantonese. The only numeric Hindi comparison found shows turbo giving back a large part of large-v3’s gain over small (22.3 versus 15.7 versus 42.4).
- Switching architecture is a separate question from stepping up inside Whisper. Parakeet v3 beats turbo and large-v3 on English WER and matches large-v3 on five European languages in the March paper, at much higher GPU RTFx, and it does not speak twelve product languages. Qwen 1.7B beats large-v3 on English and on Chinese vendor sets and loses to large-v3 on the vendor’s 30-language FLEURS average. Neither result makes turbo redundant, and neither result is a completed comparison against Whisper small on device.

### Gaps

- No full per-language table of Whisper small versus turbo versus large-v3 on the 30 product languages. The July 2026 sample covers 12 languages, 50 utterances each, and omits nl, pl, ru, uk, tr, sv, da, nb, fi, cs, el, he, id, th, vi, ro, hu, ca, hr.
- No Open ASR Leaderboard row for Whisper small or for a quantized small/base checkpoint of the kind used on Android.
- No measurement of whether turbo’s 809M weight, or its Q8 form around 0.8 GB, actually runs at voice-note latency on the same iOS and Android devices that run small today.
