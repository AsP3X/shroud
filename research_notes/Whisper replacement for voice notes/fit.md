# Whisper replacement fit for on-device voice notes

Scope is fit and migration cost for Shroud voice-note transcription as of 2026-10-27. Required bar: one on-device model, weights-only download, 30 languages (en de es fr it pt nl pl ru uk tr ar hi ja ko zh sv da nb fi cs el he id th vi ro hu ca hr), language detected once from the audio with no translate pass, accuracy not worse on any of those languages, and a download/RAM footprint that can be a phone default. Live captions are out of scope. Detailed WER tables were not re-derived. A model that misses Ukrainian, Thai, Hebrew, Arabic, Hindi, or CJK is not a replacement.

## Which 2025–2026 candidates fail a hard requirement?

### Takeaway
Every named replacement fails at least one hard requirement. The failures are language coverage first, then the lack of a runtime already integrated on iOS, Android, and web. No candidate is a drop-in for the 30-language on-device path.

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` (released on Hugging Face 2025-08-14) transcribes 25 European languages only: bg, hr, cs, da, nl, en, et, fi, fr, de, el, hu, it, lv, lt, mt, pl, pt, ro, sk, sl, es, sv, ru, uk. It auto-detects language and emits punctuation plus word- and segment-level timestamps. It does not list Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Thai, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Turkish, Catalan, or Norwegian. The card says it is ready for commercial and non-commercial use under CC-BY-4.0, needs at least 2 GB RAM to load, and is built to run on NVIDIA GPUs; a C++ path is documented via NeMo-Speech.cpp, plus NeMo and Transformers. — [nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)
- NVIDIA `parakeet-1.1b-rnnt-multilingual-asr` is a different 1.1B model. Its NIM card lists 25 languages and variants that do include Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Turkish, and both Norwegian Bokmål and Nynorsk, but the published list does not include Chinese, Ukrainian, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Greek, Finnish, Romanian, Hungarian, Catalan, or Croatian. It is under the NVIDIA Community Model License, not MIT, and the card says it is ready for commercial use. — [NVIDIA NIM model card](https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/parakeet-1_1b-rnnt-multilingual-asr/modelcard)
- `canary-1b-v2` is a ~1B FastConformer model for ASR and speech translation in the same 25 European languages as Parakeet v3 (including Ukrainian and Croatian; not Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Thai, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Turkish, Catalan, or Norwegian). CC-BY-4.0, commercial use allowed. Original `canary-1b` is only English, German, French, and Spanish and is CC-BY-NC-4.0. — [nvidia/canary-1b-v2](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b-v2); [nvidia/canary-1b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b)
- Moonshine is a family of tiny per-language models, not one multilingual detector. As of the 2026-08-24 docs, streaming MIT checkpoints exist for English, Arabic, German, Japanese, Mandarin, Spanish, Tagalog, and Vietnamese; Korean and Ukrainian still default to non-streaming models under the non-commercial Moonshine Community License. There is no auto-detect across Shroud’s 30 languages. The original 2024 card is English-only. — [Moonshine available models](https://moonshine-voice.readthedocs.io/en/stable/models/available-models/); [UsefulSensors/moonshine card](https://huggingface.co/UsefulSensors/moonshine/blob/main/README.md)
- SenseVoice Small recognizes Mandarin, Cantonese, English, Japanese, and Korean, with language, emotion, and audio-event tags in one pass. That is five languages. FunASR’s own comparison page says the whole FunASR stack is MIT and points CPU/edge use at SenseVoice. — [FunASR model guide](https://www.funasr.com/en/blog/which-funasr-model.html); [AXERA SenseVoice card](https://huggingface.co/AXERA-TECH/SenseVoice)
- Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano-2512 is an 800M checkpoint tagged for 31 languages. The model card’s list is Chinese, English, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Thai, Malay, Filipino, Arabic, Hindi, Bulgarian, Croatian, Czech, Danish, Dutch, Estonian, Finnish, Greek, Hungarian, Irish, Latvian, Lithuanian, Maltese, Polish, Portuguese, Romanian, Slovak, Slovenian, and Swedish. It does not include German, French, Spanish, Italian, Russian, Turkish, Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, or Catalan. A third-party GGUF card lists some of those missing languages; that contradicts the upstream card and is not used here. — [FunAudioLLM/Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano-2512](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano-2512)
- Kyutai ships `stt-1b-en_fr` (English and French) and `stt-2.6b-en` (English only). The official page documents PyTorch, a Rust server, and MLX for iPhone and Mac. It is a streaming model, not a 30-language note transcriber. — [Kyutai STT](https://kyutai.org/stt)
- Voxtral Small 24B-2507 (Apache-2.0) does automatic language detection and transcription for eight languages: English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Hindi, German, Dutch, and Italian. The 24B size is not a mid-range phone default. — [mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507)
- A 2026-10-20 secondary write-up describes Voxtral 3 “Small Transcribe” as covering 50 languages, with diarization and context biasing, available as an EU-hosted API or organization self-deployment. The same write-up says the announcement does not state that weights are publicly downloadable. No model card was found that clears it for the three on-device clients. — [RanThis summary](https://ranthis.com/news/mistral-voxtral-3-voice-agent-sdk)
- IBM Granite Speech 5.0 (Hugging Face blog, 2026-10-21) transcribes English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese, and Japanese. Apache-2.0. The 2B model adds language-ID prompting and keyword biasing up to 2,000 terms; the 470M Turbo CTC build is the on-device/speed variant and has a browser WebGPU demo. Six ASR languages fail the bar. — [Granite Speech 5.0 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-speech-5)
- Qwen3-ASR-1.7B and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B (released 2026-01-29, Apache-2.0) do language identification and ASR for 30 languages plus 22 Chinese dialects: zh, en, yue, ar, de, fr, es, pt, id, it, ko, ru, th, vi, ja, tr, hi, ms, nl, sv, da, fi, pl, cs, fil, fa, el, hu, mk, ro. Against Shroud’s list that misses Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian. Timestamps are a second model, Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B, limited to 11 languages (Chinese, English, Cantonese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish). Official inference is the `qwen-asr` package on Transformers or vLLM, with FlashAttention called out for GPU. — [QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR); [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1)
- Gemma 3n is an on-device multimodal model (effective 2B and 4B, audio encoder from USM, clips up to 30 seconds at launch) under the Gemma license, which requires passing a prohibited-use policy to downstream users. Google’s developer guide describes ASR and AST but only calls out strong speech-translation results between English and Spanish, French, Italian, and Portuguese. It does not document the 30 required ASR languages, a single language-ID pass, or word timestamps. — [Gemma 3n developer guide](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-gemma-3n-developer-guide/); [google/gemma-3n-E4B](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3n-E4B); [Gemma terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms.md.txt)
- sherpa-onnx Zipformer checkpoints are per language or small sets (examples on the current docs: bilingual Chinese–English, Korean, French, Japanese, Russian, Thai), not one 30-language model with a single language ID. The same runtime also loads Whisper, SenseVoice (zh/en/ja/ko/yue), English Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2, and Moonshine. — [sherpa-onnx docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/); [PyPI package](https://pypi.org/project/sherpa-onnx/)
- Whisper `large-v3-turbo` is multilingual, not English-only. Hugging Face tags the repo as 99 languages and MIT. It is large-v3 with the decoder cut from 32 layers to 4 (809M). OpenAI’s maintainer says it was fine-tuned on the large-v3 multilingual transcription data with translation data excluded, “performs similarly to large-v2”, and shows larger degradation on some languages such as Thai and Cantonese. Thai is one of Shroud’s required languages. The card also states that Whisper can emit text that was not spoken (hallucination). — [openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo); [OpenAI turbo announcement](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)
- Current whisper.cpp (repo page viewed 2026-10-19, MIT) already ships ggml files for `large-v3` (2.9 GiB), `large-v3-q5_0` (1.1 GiB), `large-v3-turbo` (1.5 GiB), and `large-v3-turbo-q5_0` (547 MiB). Its memory table lists generic “large” at about 3.9 GB. Models without `.en` are multilingual. — [whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master); [models table](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master/models)

### Inferences
- Parakeet v3, Canary-1b-v2, the 1.1B Parakeet RNNT, Canary-1B, Moonshine, SenseVoice, Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano, Kyutai STT, Voxtral Small 24B, and Granite Speech 5.0 all fail language coverage. Qwen3-ASR fails it too, specifically on Ukrainian and Hebrew, which the product bar treats as disqualifying, plus Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian.
- Zipformer is not a replacement model. It is a runtime that still needs a 30-language acoustic model Shroud does not have in one checkpoint.
- Gemma 3n fails as an ASR replacement on documented language coverage and on licence shape, and it is a general multimodal model rather than a speech decoder with the Whisper control surface.
- Voxtral 3 is not an available on-device replacement on the evidence found. Treat it as an API or private-deploy product until a weight licence and a language list are on a model card.
- The only family that already covers the 30 languages inside the three current engines is Whisper. Turbo is not a strict accuracy upgrade over large-v3: OpenAI explicitly flags extra degradation on Thai.

### Gaps
- Parakeet 1.1B’s NIM list was taken from the model-card extract. A full locale table was not opened beyond that extract, so an unlisted language is “not in the published list”, not a proof that NVIDIA trained it and forgot to mention it.
- Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano’s Hugging Face tag says Apache-2.0. A downstream GGUF card claims the weights use FunASR Model License v1.1. The weight `LICENSE` file was not opened, so the licence conflict is unresolved, but the language list alone already fails the model.
- Kyutai’s licence was not on the official page extract. A third-party operator page says CC-BY-4.0. Not used as a licence finding.
- Whether whisper.cpp 1.9.4, the Android pin named in the task, loads turbo was not checked against that tag. Current master does.
- WhisperKit’s current release was not confirmed from Argmax source. Mintlify mirrors dated 2026-03 list variants through `large-v3` and a separate English-oriented Distil-Large-v3, and do not list turbo. Those mirrors look generated. Do not treat “WhisperKit cannot load turbo” as established.
- transformers.js model coverage was not re-opened. The task states the web client already uses it for Whisper.
- No primary model card was found for a single public checkpoint that covers all 30 languages outside Whisper.

## Are the licences usable in a commercial mobile app?

### Takeaway
MIT (Whisper, whisper.cpp) and Apache-2.0 (Qwen3-ASR, Granite, Voxtral Small 24B) are compatible with shipping a mobile app. CC-BY-4.0 (Parakeet v3, Canary-1b-v2) allows commercial use but requires attribution. CC-BY-NC, the Moonshine Community License, the Gemma use-restriction flow-down, and the unread NVIDIA Community License are the ones that do not match a plain MIT dependency. Language failures make most of the permissive models irrelevant anyway.

### Cited Findings
- `whisper-large-v3-turbo` is tagged MIT on Hugging Face. whisper.cpp’s repository is MIT. — [turbo model](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo); [whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master)
- `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` and `canary-1b-v2` are CC-BY-4.0 and marked ready for commercial use. CC-BY-4.0 is attribution, not a non-commercial or field-of-use ban. — [Parakeet v3 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3); [Canary-1b-v2 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b-v2)
- Original `canary-1b` is CC-BY-NC-4.0. — [nvidia/canary-1b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b)
- The 1.1B multilingual Parakeet NIM card says use is governed by the NVIDIA Community Model License and that the model is ready for commercial use. The licence text itself was not read. — [NIM card](https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/parakeet-1_1b-rnnt-multilingual-asr/modelcard)
- Moonshine docs: streaming models are MIT; older non-streaming models for languages other than English stay under the Moonshine Community License, which the docs describe as non-commercial. Korean and Ukrainian still default to those Community checkpoints. — [available models](https://moonshine-voice.readthedocs.io/en/stable/models/available-models/)
- Qwen3-ASR’s repository is Apache-2.0. The technical report says the models are released under Apache-2.0. — [QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR); [report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1)
- Granite Speech 5.0 models are Apache-2.0. — [IBM blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-speech-5)
- Voxtral Small 24B-2507 is Apache-2.0. The Voxtral paper says both Mini and Small are Apache-2.0. — [model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507); [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13264)
- Gemma 3n weights use the Gemma license. Section 3.1 requires the use restrictions to be an enforceable term for anyone the weights are distributed to. Section 3.2 incorporates the prohibited-use policy (illegal activity, scams, a range of sexual content, and other categories) and lets Google restrict usage it believes violates the agreement. That is not MIT, and it is not a non-commercial ban either. — [terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms.md.txt); [prohibited use policy](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/prohibited_use_policy); [model card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3n-E4B)
- SenseVoice on the AXERA card is tagged MIT. FunASR’s 2026 model-selection post says the FunASR stack, including SenseVoice and Fun-ASR-Nano / MLT-Nano, is MIT. The MLT-Nano Hugging Face header is tagged Apache-2.0, which disagrees with both a “MIT weights” reading and the third-party FunASR Model License v1.1 claim. — [AXERA card](https://huggingface.co/AXERA-TECH/SenseVoice); [FunASR guide](https://www.funasr.com/en/blog/which-funasr-model.html); [MLT-Nano card](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano-2512); [GGUF repackage](https://huggingface.co/cstr/funasr-mlt-nano-GGUF)

### Inferences
- A licence problem is decisive only for original Canary-1B (non-commercial), non-streaming Moonshine language packs (non-commercial), and, as a distribution cost, Gemma (use restrictions must ship with the app). Those models already fail language coverage.
- CC-BY-4.0 would be acceptable for a commercial messenger if the model were otherwise fit. Parakeet v3 and Canary-1b-v2 are not otherwise fit.
- Apache-2.0 on Qwen3-ASR would be acceptable. The language holes and the GPU-first runtime are what block it, not the licence.
- Do not ship Fun-ASR weights until the weight licence file is read. The public tags disagree.

### Gaps
- NVIDIA Community Model License full text, FunASR Model License v1.1, and the Moonshine Community License legal text were not opened. “Non-commercial” for Moonshine is taken from the project’s own docs page, not from a lawyer’s reading of the license file.
- sherpa-onnx’s repository licence file was not opened. Model weights it repackages keep their own licences; a 2026 models-registry note says its licence field is best-effort and must be checked per checkpoint. — [sherpa-onnx-models crate](https://crates.io/crates/sherpa-onnx-models)
- No OpenRAIL model in this candidate list was confirmed from a model card. Absence here is not a claim that none exist.

## What is the maintenance risk, and what do production apps ship?

### Takeaway
Staying on Whisper keeps one model family in three runtimes that already exist (WhisperKit, whisper.cpp, transformers.js). Moving to sherpa-onnx or to ONNX-per-OS exports replaces that with a second stack and still does not yield a 30-language model. Public 2026 evidence does not show Signal, Telegram, or WhatsApp shipping a named open ASR model on device. Offline apps that do name models keep Whisper for the long tail and use Parakeet only where 25 European languages are enough.

### Cited Findings
- whisper.cpp is a maintained C/C++ port (about 54.6k stars on the 2026-10-19 repository view), MIT, with preconverted multilingual ggml files including quantized large-v3 and turbo. The same binary is the class of runtime Shroud already uses on Android. — [whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master)
- sherpa-onnx’s documentation index, updated 2026-10-27, offers Android, iOS, HarmonyOS, Flutter, and WebAssembly builds, and a model list that mixes streaming Zipformer, SenseVoice, Whisper, Moonshine, English Parakeet, and FireRed. Prebuilt examples on the Flutter page are per-model demos (SenseVoice for zh/en/ja/ko/yue, Whisper tiny.en, Zipformer bilingual), not one multilingual messenger pipeline. — [docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/); [Flutter examples](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/master/flutter/sherpa_onnx/example/example.md)
- A third-party code walk dated 2026-10-27 says the sherpa-onnx tree also contains Qwen3-ASR. The official docs extract reviewed here did not list Qwen3-ASR in the pretrained-model tables. Treat support as unverified. — [PyShine walkthrough](https://pyshine.com/sherpa-onnx/)
- Anti-Vocale, an F-Droid app updated 2026-10-25 that transcribes voice notes from WhatsApp, Telegram, and other apps entirely on device, ships Parakeet TDT for 25 European languages (862 MB or 464 MB), Whisper Turbo for 99 languages, and also lists Qwen3-ASR, Nemotron streaming, and Gemma. The app is not a messenger, and its “59 languages” line for Qwen3-ASR does not match Qwen’s own 30-plus-22-dialects table. — [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/packages/com.antivocale.app/)
- Google Play app “Audio to Text”, updated 2026-09-29, transcribes shared voice messages locally with sherpa-onnx and says the only network use is the model download. It is a share-sheet utility, not WhatsApp or Telegram. — [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.b0sh.audiotext&hl=en_US)
- No Signal support article or Signal blog announcing on-device voice-note transcription was found. Secondary pages in August 2026 state that Signal has no built-in transcription and that users export audio to another app. Those pages are not Signal. — [Audio Notes](https://getaudionote.com/blog/transcribe-signal-voice-messages); [Transcribbit comparison](https://transcribbit.io/blog/messaging-app-accessibility)
- WhatsApp’s own engineering post naming the ASR model was not found. WABetaInfo (2026-09-18) describes Android voice-to-text that uses locally downloaded language packs, offline, and says those are the same packs as voice-message transcription. It does not name Whisper or any open model. An older secondary article repeats the “on device, language pack” claim without a model name. — [WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-is-testing-a-new-voice-to-text-feature-for-messages/)
- Telegram’s own post was not found. A secondary comparison says Telegram transcription is server-side, with a free-tier cap and unlimited use on Premium. That would violate Shroud’s default path even if the language coverage were fine. — [Transcribbit](https://transcribbit.io/blog/messaging-app-accessibility)
- Parakeet v3’s own card prefers NVIDIA GPUs and Linux, documents NeMo and Transformers, and adds NeMo-Speech.cpp. It does not document WhisperKit, whisper.cpp, or transformers.js. — [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)
- Qwen3-ASR’s official quickstart is Python with Transformers or vLLM, and streaming is vLLM-only. There is no official iOS or Android library in that README. — [QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)

### Inferences
- The maintenance choice is not “Whisper versus a better model in the same three engines”. None of the replacements load in all three engines today. The real fork is “new Whisper checkpoint in whisper.cpp / WhisperKit / transformers.js” versus “adopt sherpa-onnx or a new export pipeline on three operating systems, then still fail the language bar”.
- Production offline transcribers in 2026 that publish a model list use Whisper Turbo when they need roughly 100 languages, and Parakeet when the user is inside the 25 European languages. That matches the constraint, rather than contradicting it.
- Adopting sherpa-onnx would add a fourth runtime (or replace three) and a matrix of per-model licences. It would not remove Whisper, because the Zipformer and SenseVoice checkpoints do not cover the 30 languages.
- Messenger comparisons do not justify a switch. Signal appears, on secondary sources only, not to ship transcription. WhatsApp’s documented behavior is on-device language packs with no public model name. Telegram, if the secondary description is right, is server-side and therefore the wrong architecture for Shroud.

### Gaps
- Primary sources for Signal, Telegram, and WhatsApp ASR were not found. Do not write that any of them uses Whisper, Parakeet, or the OS speech API. The negative claim for Signal is only as strong as two secondary blogs.
- WhisperKit turbo support and transformers.js turbo/large-v3 ONNX support were not re-verified in vendor repos.
- Whether NeMo-Speech.cpp or sherpa-onnx Parakeet builds run at acceptable RAM on mid-range Android and iOS was not measured. Parakeet’s “2 GB to load” figure is from the NVIDIA card and is not a phone benchmark.

## Is an in-family Whisper upgrade better than an architecture change?

### Takeaway
Yes. Upgrade inside Whisper. An architecture change fails language coverage or throws away three existing integrations, or both. Between checkpoints, full large-v3 is the accuracy-safe in-family model and a poor phone default at 2.9 GiB; turbo is the size and speed model OpenAI actually ships for multilingual transcription, with an explicit warning of extra errors on Thai versus large-v3.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI: turbo has 4 decoder layers instead of 32, was fine-tuned on large-v3’s multilingual transcription set, excludes translation data, performs similarly to large-v2 across languages, and degrades more on Thai and Cantonese. It is not Distil-Whisper. Distil-Whisper distilled a smaller model; turbo was fine-tuned, and the strong Distil checkpoints remain the English-only line the current plan already rejected. — [discussion #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)
- Hugging Face: turbo is multilingual (the size table marks large-v3-turbo as multilingual only, 809M) and the card’s limitation text still says predictions may include text that was not spoken. The card’s usage sample still shows `task: translate`, which conflicts with OpenAI’s statement that turbo was not trained on translation and should not be expected to translate well. Shroud does not want a translate pass either way. — [model card](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)
- whisper.cpp file sizes: large-v3 2.9 GiB, large-v3-q5_0 1.1 GiB, large-v3-turbo 1.5 GiB, large-v3-turbo-q5_0 547 MiB. The 2.9 GiB file matches the “multi-gigabyte is a poor default” constraint. The 547 MiB and 1.1 GiB files do not, as downloads. RAM on device was not measured; the upstream table’s ~3.9 GB figure is for full “large”. — [models README](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master/models)
- Parakeet v3 publishes its own multilingual WER tables (FLEURS average 11.97%, MLS 7.83%, CoVoST 11.98%, English Open ASR average 6.34%). Those numbers are not comparable to a Shroud voice-note set, and they only cover languages Shroud already has in Whisper plus some European languages Shroud does not require. They do not cover the languages Parakeet is missing. — [Parakeet v3 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)
- Qwen’s README claims the 1.7B model is state-of-the-art among open-source ASR models and competitive with commercial APIs. That claim is on Qwen’s benchmarks and does not include Ukrainian or Hebrew. The 0.6B model is described as the accuracy/efficiency tradeoff. Neither claim repairs the missing languages or the missing mobile runtimes. — [Qwen3-ASR README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)

### Inferences
- Architecture change is not justified. The models people would switch to are either European-only, CJK-heavy with European holes, English-bilingual, or six-to-eight languages, and none of them already run in WhisperKit, whisper.cpp, and transformers.js.
- If the goal is “better Whisper without a new stack”, the candidate is a newer Whisper checkpoint in the existing engines. large-v3 is the one that does not add turbo’s Thai penalty. turbo is the one that fits a phone if quantization is acceptable, and it must be rejected for any language where it is worse than the checkpoint Shroud ships today. OpenAI’s own note means Thai cannot be assumed safe against large-v3.
- Quantized large-v3 (q5_0, 1.1 GiB) is the size compromise that keeps the 32-layer decoder. Quantization error versus fp16 was not measured, so it is not yet known to satisfy “no language gets worse”.
- Distil-Whisper stays rejected. Turbo does not reopen that decision; it is still Whisper, still multilingual, and still not a licence or runtime change.

### Gaps
- Shroud’s current checkpoint (tiny, base, small, medium, or large) was not read from product code. Whether turbo is an upgrade or a regression depends on that baseline. Against large-v3, OpenAI says it is not uniformly better. Against a smaller model, it may be better and still worse on Thai than large-v3.
- No on-device WER or silence-hallucination measurement for q5_0 turbo versus q5_0 large-v3 was found in the sources used here.
- Core ML and ONNX exports of turbo were not confirmed in this pass.

## What would a migration break?

### Takeaway
Leaving Whisper drops the shared control surface the three clients already have: one language-ID pass with a probability, decoder-side timestamps, a text prompt for names, and the silence/hallucination thresholds. The closest models do not offer that surface on all 30 languages, and several need a second model or a second pass Shroud has ruled out.

### Cited Findings
- Whisper predicts the audio language automatically. The Hugging Face card shows forcing a language, returning segment timestamps, and returning word timestamps. It also documents `no_speech_threshold`, `logprob_threshold`, and `compression_ratio_threshold` as generation inputs. The limitation section states that weakly supervised training produces text that was not in the audio. — [turbo card](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo)
- OpenAI says turbo was not trained on translation. A translate pass is both unwanted for Shroud and unsupported as a quality feature on turbo. — [discussion #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)
- A comment on that same thread reports `no_speech_prob` near zero on turbo while large still produced a usable value. The maintainer did not confirm or reject that report in the portion of the thread that was read. It is a user report, not a verified turbo defect. — [discussion #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)
- Parakeet v3 auto-detects language, includes punctuation and capitalization, and returns word- and segment-level timestamps in NeMo. The card does not document a text prompt, contact-name biasing, or a language-probability vector. Long-audio settings cited there (24 minutes full attention, 3 hours local attention) are on an A100 80 GB, not a phone. — [Parakeet v3 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)
- Qwen3-ASR returns a language string and can be forced with `language=`. Timestamps require loading ForcedAligner-0.6B in addition to the ASR model, and that aligner supports 11 languages, not the ASR set and not Shroud’s 30. Streaming does not return timestamps. — [Qwen3-ASR README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR)
- Granite Speech 5.0-2B documents prompt-based keyword biasing up to 2,000 terms, which is the feature closest to contact-name priming. It only transcribes six languages. — [Granite Speech 5.0 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-speech-5)
- Canary-1b-v2’s task list includes speech translation as a first-class mode. Using that mode would be the second pass Shroud forbids. Transcription-only use would still lack Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, CJK, Thai, and the other holes listed above. — [canary-1b-v2](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b-v2)
- SenseVoice emits punctuation as part of the non-autoregressive pass, but only for zh/yue/en/ja/ko. FunASR’s selection guide points Chinese hotwords and timestamps at Paraformer, not at SenseVoice. — [FunASR guide](https://www.funasr.com/en/blog/which-funasr-model.html)

### Inferences
- Timestamps: Whisper word and segment timestamps are in the model API the three clients can already call. Parakeet has them in NeMo, but not for the missing languages. Qwen’s timestamps are a second 0.6B model and drop most of Shroud’s languages. A migration that “keeps timestamps” is not free.
- Language ID: Shroud detects once and decodes in that language. Whisper does that inside one model. Qwen can too, on its own 30 languages, without Ukrainian or Hebrew. Parakeet v3 auto-detects inside 25 European languages. None of the others both detect and cover the list.
- Contact-name biasing: Whisper’s decoder prompt is the existing mechanism. Granite has a real keyword list and the wrong languages. Parakeet v3 and Qwen’s quickstart do not document an equivalent. Replacing Whisper drops name biasing unless a new mechanism is built and tested per language.
- Punctuation: Parakeet and Canary bake it in. Whisper’s punctuation is part of the same decode the product already accepted when it rejected a second punctuation model. Switching would change punctuation behavior with no evidence it is better on the 30 languages.
- Silence hallucinations: the failure mode is documented for Whisper, and the thresholds to suppress it are part of the Whisper generate API. No reviewed replacement card claims those thresholds or a measured zero rate on silence. A transducer such as Parakeet may behave differently; that is not a source-backed claim and is not a reason to switch.
- Per-platform parity: one Whisper checkpoint can be exported to ggml, Core ML, and ONNX. A new architecture means three new exports or one new runtime on three OSes, then a 30-language parity test that the new model cannot pass because it does not contain the languages.

### Gaps
- The exact flag names Shroud uses for `initial_prompt`, language-probability thresholds, and word timestamps were not read from Shroud code. The findings above are about the public model APIs, not a diff against the app.
- No source in this pass measured how often turbo hallucinates on silence relative to large-v3. The user report about `no_speech_prob` is unverified.
- Word-timestamp quality differences across whisper.cpp, WhisperKit, and transformers.js were not compared.

## Verdict: stay, upgrade inside Whisper, or switch?

### Takeaway
Stay on Whisper and upgrade inside the family. Do not switch. No 2025–2026 alternative survives the 30-language, on-device, single-pass, phone-default constraints at once. The single best alternative does not exist yet; Qwen3-ASR-0.6B is the nearest permissive model and it still misses Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian and has no official three-client runtime.

### Cited Findings
- Language lists, licences, and runtimes for Parakeet v3, Parakeet 1.1B RNNT, Canary-1b-v2, Canary-1B, Moonshine, SenseVoice, Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano, Kyutai, Voxtral Small, Granite Speech 5.0, Qwen3-ASR, Gemma 3n, and sherpa-onnx Zipformer are the findings in the sections above. Each fails coverage, licence fit for a stock mobile dependency, or an on-device runtime already on all three clients. Most fail coverage. — sources cited above
- whisper.cpp already distributes large-v3 and turbo, including q5_0 files of 1.1 GiB and 547 MiB, under MIT. — [models table](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/tree/master/models)
- OpenAI says turbo is similar to large-v2 and worse on Thai and Cantonese than the large-v3 comparison they plotted, and that it was not trained to translate. — [discussion #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)

### Inferences
- Decision: upgrade inside Whisper. Do not adopt Parakeet, Canary, Moonshine, SenseVoice, Fun-ASR, Kyutai, Voxtral, Granite, Qwen, Gemma, or a Zipformer stack as the voice-note engine.
- Practical order inside Whisper: keep the current hallucination thresholds; evaluate quantized large-v3 if the product can spend about 1.1 GiB and wants the unpruned decoder; evaluate turbo, especially the 547 MiB q5_0 file, only as a speed and download option that is not allowed to ship if Thai or any other required language gets worse than today’s checkpoint. Full 2.9 GiB large-v3 is a poor default on mid-range phones.
- If a later Qwen checkpoint adds Ukrainian, Hebrew, Norwegian, Catalan, and Croatian, publishes word timestamps without a second 11-language model, and gains maintained iOS, Android, and web runtimes, reopen the switch. That checkpoint was not found on 2026-10-27.

### Gaps
- Current Shroud checkpoint id and measured per-language error were not in this pass, so “turbo is an upgrade” is not a supported sentence. “Turbo is the same stack and might be faster” is supported. “Turbo is no worse on every required language” is not, because OpenAI called out Thai.
- Phone RAM for q5_0 turbo and q5_0 large-v3 on mid-range Android and iOS was not measured.
- Assumptions used: the three clients already run Whisper and can take a new official checkpoint without a new decoder API; voice notes are short enough that server-style long-audio tricks are irrelevant; commercial distribution rules out NC and research-only weights; attribution-only CC-BY would have been acceptable if coverage had passed; Voxtral 3 is out until weights and a language list are public; secondary messenger articles are not evidence of which model those apps run; no WER number from a leaderboard was used to declare a winner.
