# Open multilingual speech-to-text models as Whisper replacements

Scope: open-weight ASR models that could replace Whisper for on-device voice-note transcription, emphasizing releases and material updates from 2025 through 2026-10-27. Models from 2024 and earlier are marked as prior art. Deployment runtimes are out of scope except where a model card names ONNX, GGML/GGUF, Core ML, or a similar export. No WER, speed, or size figure below was estimated; if a number is absent from the cited source it is listed under Gaps.

Reference set of 30 language codes used throughout: en, de, es, fr, it, pt, nl, pl, ru, uk, tr, ar, hi, ja, ko, zh, sv, da, nb, fi, cs, el, he, id, th, vi, ro, hu, ca, hr.

Whisper baseline (prior art, still the bar these models are compared with):

- OpenAI Whisper large-v3 is recorded on the Open ASR Leaderboard as a 99-language model, 1.55B parameters in the Distil-Whisper comparison table (short-form WER 8.4, long-form 11.0 on that org’s own table; leaderboard English average WER 7.44 and RTFx 145.51 as of the 8 Oct 2025 paper snapshot). — [Distil-Whisper org card](https://huggingface.co/distil-whisper); [Open ASR Leaderboard paper, 8 Oct 2025 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)
- Whisper large-v3-turbo reduced the decoder from 32 layers to 4 by distillation. The LiteASR paper states that fact and does not restate a parameter count. — [LiteASR](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20583v2)
- The public leaderboard’s multilingual tab is only German, French, Italian, Spanish, and Portuguese (FLEURS, CoVoST-2, MLS; Hindi is a separate Voice Arena set). It is not a 30-language scoreboard. The space was still being updated in October 2026 (Gradio “last updated 22 October 2026”). — [Leaderboard README](https://github.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard/blob/main/README.md); [Gradio space](https://hf-audio-open-asr-leaderboard.hf.space/); [constants / EU language set](https://hf-mirror.com/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard/raw/main/constants.py)

## Which open-weight models are realistic successors or competitors to Whisper small, medium, large-v3, and large-v3-turbo?

### Takeaway

No 2025–2026 model retrieved here was verified to match Whisper large-v3 on all four of: permissive licence, all 30 target languages, language identification, punctuation, and timestamps, at a size comparable to Whisper small or medium. The realistic open-weight field splits into a broad-coverage group (Meta Omnilingual ASR; ESPnet OWSM v4), a fast European group that misses Asian and several other codes (NVIDIA Canary-1B-v2 and Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3), and a strong but incomplete multilingual group led by Qwen3-ASR (30 languages plus 22 Chinese dialects, missing uk, nb, he, ca, and hr). Voxtral Small/Mini (July 2025) beat Whisper where they overlap but ship only about eight languages; Voxtral 3 (October 2026) claims 50+ languages and does not publish weights.

### Cited Findings

#### Meta Omnilingual ASR (2025) — broadest open coverage

- Released 10 November 2025 by Meta FAIR. Family from compact 300M variants “for low-power devices” to 7B models. Apache 2.0 weights. More than 1,600 languages, including about 500 the authors say had never been served by an ASR system. 7B LLM-ASR reports character error rate below 10 for 78% of those languages. — [Meta blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/omnilingual-asr-advancing-automatic-speech-recognition/); [Meta research page](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/omnilingual-asr-open-source-multilingual-speech-recognition-for-1600-languages/); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09690v1)
- Model card for `omniASR-LLM-300M` lists FP32 download sizes for SSL encoders: W2V 300M = 317,390,592 params / 1.2 GiB; W2V 1B = 965,514,752 / 3.6 GiB; W2V 3B = 3,064,124,672 / 12.0 GiB. Full language list is programmatic (`lang_ids.py` in facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr), not printed on the card. — [facebook/omniASR-LLM-300M README](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-300M/raw/main/README.md)
- On the Open ASR Leaderboard multilingual track (de/fr/it/es/pt only), as of 27 March 2026: Omnilingual ASR LLM 7B v2 average WER 4.39, RTFx 21.2; Omnilingual ASR LLM 7B average WER 4.68, RTFx 21.8; Whisper large-v3 average WER 4.81, RTFx 111. So the 7B is slightly more accurate than Whisper on those five languages and several times slower on the leaderboard’s RTFx. — [Interspeech 2026 leaderboard paper PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/srivastav26_interspeech.pdf)
- Training corpus “AllASR” is described by a secondary write-up as 120,710 hours across 1,690 languages. That hour count is from MarkTechPost, not copied from the Meta blog extract. — [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/11/11/meta-ai-releases-omnilingual-asr-a-suite-of-open-source-multilingual-speech-recognition-models-for-1600-languages/)

#### Qwen3-ASR (2026) — best verified fit among dedicated sub-2B multilingual ASR models, with five gaps

- Qwen team, Alibaba Cloud. Technical report arXiv:2601.21337. GitHub repo surfaced as published 29 January 2026. Apache 2.0. Two ASR models, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B, plus Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B. Authors: language identification and ASR for 52 languages and dialects, specifically 30 languages and 22 Chinese dialects, built on Qwen3-Omni. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1); [GitHub README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/main/README.md); [GitHub repo](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/)
- The 30 languages named on the README: Chinese (zh), English (en), Cantonese (yue), Arabic (ar), German (de), French (fr), Spanish (es), Portuguese (pt), Indonesian (id), Italian (it), Korean (ko), Russian (ru), Thai (th), Vietnamese (vi), Japanese (ja), Turkish (tr), Hindi (hi), Malay (ms), Dutch (nl), Swedish (sv), Danish (da), Finnish (fi), Polish (pl), Czech (cs), Filipino (fil), Persian (fa), Greek (el), Hungarian (hu), Macedonian (mk), Romanian (ro). — [Qwen3-ASR README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/main/README.md)
- Forced aligner supports timestamp prediction; the report says timestamp accuracy beats “the three strongest force alignment models” and that the aligner covers 11 languages. Streaming and offline share one model. Authors say the 1.7B is state-of-the-art among open-source ASR and competitive with the strongest proprietary APIs, and that the 0.6B has the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off. One reported efficiency line: Qwen3-ASR-0.6B average time-to-first-token as low as 92 ms, and “transcribe 2000 seconds speech in 1 second at a concurrency of 128.” — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1); [ResearchGate abstract mirror](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400236592_Qwen3-ASR_Technical_Report)
- Their open-benchmark table (WER, lower better), column order GLM-ASR-Nano-2512, Whisper-large-v3, Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano, Qwen3-ASR-0.6B, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B:
  - MLS: 13.32, 8.62, 28.70, 13.19, **8.55**
  - CommonVoice: 19.40, 10.77, 17.25, 12.75, **9.18**
  - MLC-SLM: 34.93, 15.68, 29.94, 15.84, **12.74**
  - Fleurs: 16.08, 5.27, 10.03, 7.57, **4.90**
  - Fleurs†: 20.05, 6.85, 31.89, 10.37, **6.62**
  - Fleurs††: 24.83, **8.16**, 47.84, 21.80, 12.60
  - Whisper large-v3 wins the Fleurs†† slice. Qwen3-ASR-1.7B wins the other five rows in that table. — [GitHub README extract](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/)
- Language-ID accuracy (percent): Fleurs Whisper-large-v3 94.6, Qwen3-ASR-0.6B 97.1, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B 98.7. MLS is a tie at 99.9 between Whisper and the 1.7B. — [GitHub README extract](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/)
- A secondary page says the transformers-native HF repos were created 26 June 2026 and guesses ~4 GB fp16 for the 1.7B and ~1.5–2 GB for the 0.6B. Those sizes are not from the technical report. The same page says Qwen3-ASR-Flash is API-only. — [The AI Bench](https://theaibench.ai/changes/drops/2026-06-26-qwen3-asr/)
- Related, not dedicated ASR: Qwen3-Omni (Apache 2.0 for the released 30B-A3B checkpoints) supports speech understanding in 19 languages: ar, de, en, es, fr, id, it, ja, ko, ms, nl, pt, ru, th, tr, ur, vi, yue, zh. Text in 119 languages; speech generation in 10. Authors claim open-source SOTA on 32 of 36 audio and audio-visual benchmarks. This is a large omni model, not a Whisper-small-class recognizer. — [Qwen3-Omni technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17765v1); [Qwen blog excerpt](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=fdfbaf2907a36b7659a470c77fb135e381302028)

#### NVIDIA Canary-1B-v2 and Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 (2025) — fastest serious European models

- Paper submitted 17 September 2025 (revised 25 September 2025): Canary-1B-v2 is a FastConformer encoder plus Transformer decoder for ASR and speech-to-text translation, “25 languages primarily European,” trained on 1.7M hours including Granary and NeMo ASR Set 3.0. Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 is the 600M successor to the English-only v2 and covers the same 25 languages. Licence on the Parakeet card is CC-BY-4.0. — [arXiv:2509.14128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14128); [nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)
- Official Parakeet language list: bg, hr, cs, da, nl, en, et, fi, fr, de, el, hu, it, lv, lt, mt, pl, pt, ro, sk, sl, es, sv, ru, uk. The card describes it as a 600-million-parameter model extending English-only parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2, with long-audio support up to 24 minutes full attention (A100 80GB) or up to 3 hours with local attention. HF file sizes observed: `model.safetensors` 2.51 GB and `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3.nemo` 2.51 GB. — [NVIDIA model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3); [safetensors blob](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3/blob/593ce355afbff63a6412af0a395e635065cc0fc0/model.safetensors); [.nemo blob](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3/blob/47a82b3739007fd408a647bbd72560ab9e9e8ec2/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3.nemo)
- A community GGUF pack (CC-BY-4.0, not NVIDIA’s own quant) lists F16 1.26 GB, Q8_0 711 MB, Q5_0 516 MB, Q4_K 467 MB, and states word-level timestamps come from the TDT decoder with no separate CTC aligner, plus “6.34% avg WER” on the HF Open ASR Leaderboard. An OpenASR repack says the model auto-detects language and preserves punctuation and capitalization; its fp16 `.oasr` pack is 1.42 GB and q8_0 is 919 MB. — [cstr GGUF card](https://huggingface.co/cstr/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-GGUF); [OpenASR pack](https://huggingface.co/OpenASR/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)
- Author claim in the paper abstract: Canary-1B-v2 “outperforms Whisper-large-v3 on English ASR while being 10x faster,” and is competitive on multilingual ASR and AST against SeamlessM4T-v2-large and LLM-based systems. A paper-summary page attributes these leaderboard-aligned figures to the work: English WER 7.15% for Canary-1B-v2 vs 7.44% for Whisper-large-v3, RTFx 749 vs 145.51; multilingual average 8.1% WER across 24 languages vs Whisper-large-v3 9.9% and SeamlessM4T-medium 12.0%; Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 English WER 6.32% and RTFx 3332.74. The 7.15 / 749 and 6.32 / 3332.74 figures also appear in the 8 Oct 2025 Open ASR Leaderboard paper table, which is the safer citation for those two pairs. The 8.1 vs 9.9 figure was only seen on the summary page, not in the arXiv abstract extract. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14128); [alphaXiv summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.14128); [leaderboard paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)
- Leaderboard multilingual snapshot 27 March 2026: Canary 1B v2 average WER 4.60, RTFx 634, against Whisper large-v3 4.81 and RTFx 111, on de/fr/it/es/pt only. Per language in that table: Canary DE 4.10, FR 4.83, IT 4.88, ES 3.25, PT 6.33; Whisper DE 4.26, FR 6.36, IT 4.69, ES 3.65, PT 4.96. Canary is worse than Whisper on Portuguese in this row. — [Interspeech 2026 PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/srivastav26_interspeech.pdf)
- Parakeet v2 remains the English-only predecessor. On the long-form slice of the 8 Oct 2025 paper, Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 average WER is 6.91 and RTFx 955.87. v3 is the multilingual expansion, not an English accuracy upgrade. — [leaderboard paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)
- Same leaderboard table lists other NVIDIA rows that are not 25-language models: Canary 1B Flash, average English WER 6.35, RTFx 1045.75, 4 languages; Canary 1B, WER 6.50, RTFx 235.34, 4 languages; Parakeet CTC 1.1B, WER 7.40, RTFx 2728.52, 1 language. — [leaderboard paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)
- Moondream “Parakeet Ultra” / `moondream/parakeet-redux`: a 27 October 2026 third-party blog says Moondream released a further-trained Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 on 22 September 2026, same 0.6B and the same 25 languages, and reports its own M5-Mac WERs (aggregate 13.99% to 10.63%, “about a quarter fewer mistakes”). An ONNX conversion repo lists `moondream/parakeet-redux` as the base model and says the conversion inherits CC-BY-4.0. This is not an NVIDIA result and was not checked against a Moondream model card. — [Whisper Notes blog](https://whispernotes.app/blog/parakeet-ultra-vs-parakeet-v3); [ONNX README](https://huggingface.co/Olicorne/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-redux-onnx/blob/main/README.md)

#### Mistral Voxtral (open weights July 2025; Voxtral 3 is not downloadable)

- 15 July 2025: Voxtral Small 24B and Voxtral Mini 3B, Apache 2.0, both on Hugging Face. Authors: “Voxtral comprehensively outperforms Whisper large-v3.” Default mode auto-detects language. Context: up to 30 minutes for transcription, 40 minutes for understanding, 32k tokens. Named languages on the announcement and on the Small 24B card: English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Hindi, German, Dutch, Italian. The HF card is tagged “8 languages.” FLEURS evaluation text also lists Arabic alongside those eight; the card’s supported-language sentence does not include Arabic. — [Mistral announcement](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral/); [Voxtral-Small-24B-2507 card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507)
- Leaderboard, 8 Oct 2025: Voxtral Mini 3B English average WER 7.05, RTFx 109.86, 8 languages, versus Whisper large-v3 7.44 / 145.51. Multilingual de/fr/it/es/pt as of 27 March 2026: Voxtral Small 24B average WER 3.70, RTFx 42.0 (DE 3.01, FR 4.13, IT 3.91, ES 3.04, PT 4.40), the best open model in that table and ahead of Whisper large-v3 at 4.81. An earlier 8 Oct 2025 HTML table had Phi-4 and Canary ahead of a Voxtral Mini row (Mini DE 5.36, FR 5.96, IT 5.88, ES 3.81, PT 4.80). The two snapshots are different models and dates; both are reported rather than merged. — [leaderboard paper v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1); [Interspeech 2026 PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/srivastav26_interspeech.pdf)
- Voxtral 3, announced 22 October 2026: Voxtral Mini Transcribe 3 is described as 4B parameters at $0.003 per minute wait, the announcement text says $0.0035/min; Small Transcribe 3 is a larger batch model at $0.0040/min; Mini Transcribe Realtime 3 is streaming. Authors: 50+ languages including 24 official EU languages and “many low-resource Asian and Indic languages,” diarization, context biasing; Small “outperforms Whisper, Scribe v2 and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe.” Language codes printed with the figure include AR, BN, BG, CA, ZH, CS, DA, NL, EN, ET, FI, FR, DE, EL, GU, HI, HU, IS, ID, IT, JA, KN, KO, LV, LT, MK, MS, ML, MR, NO, FA, PL, PT, RO, RU, SK, SL, ES, SV, FIL, TE, TH, TR, UK, VI (VI is starred in the source). Hebrew and Croatian are not in that printed list. A secondary review states there are no public Voxtral 3 weights; on-prem is via Mistral sales. One open-weight side release named by that review: Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime Arabic, 8 October 2026, Apache 2.0, Arabic rather than a general multilingual model. — [Mistral Voxtral 3](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3/); [Reflekt lab review](https://reflektlab.ai/blog/voxtral-3/)

#### ESPnet OWSM v4 (2025) — fully open Whisper-style models, 151 languages

- OWSM v4 paper: arXiv:2506.00338, 31 May 2025, Interspeech 2025 (best student paper, per the model card). Encoder-decoder sizes: base 102M, small 370M, medium 1.02B. CTC medium 1.01B. Trained on public data, 320k hours total in the model-card wording, 151 languages (same language count the paper table gives for v3.1 and v4). Tasks on the small-model card: language identification, speech recognition, speech translation, utterance-level timestamp prediction, long-form. — [ESPnet OWSM v4 README](https://github.com/espnet/espnet/tree/master/egs2/owsm_v4/s2t1); [owsm_v4_small_370M card](https://huggingface.co/espnet/owsm_v4_small_370M/blob/main/README.md); [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00338)
- Author comparisons (paper): OWSM v4 medium lower average WER than Whisper-medium on the reported multilingual average, 9.4% vs 9.7%, at similar speed. A secondary table transcription of MLS gives OWSM v4 medium average 9.4 (eng 6.4, spa 5.7, fra 7.8, deu 8.2, nld 13.4, ita 13.1, por 9.0, pol 11.5) vs Whisper-medium 9.7. OWSM-CTC v4 English Open ASR Leaderboard average WER 7.44 and RTFx 791.18 vs Whisper-large-v3 7.47 and RTFx 235.61 in the paper’s Table 7 transcription. OWSM-CTC v4 “outperforms v3.1 across all 102 languages” on FLEURS. The paper also says OWSM-CTC v4 WER 10.7% vs MMS-fl102 17.6% and MMS-all 10.6%, “while being 20% faster.” — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00338); [AIModels.fyi table transcription](https://www.aimodels.fyi/papers/arxiv/owsm-v4-improving-open-whisper-style-speech)
- Weights are on Hugging Face under `espnet/owsm_v4_*`. This pass did not open the licence field.

#### Microsoft Phi-4-multimodal (2025) — strong, eight audio languages

- `microsoft/Phi-4-multimodal-instruct`, model card published 3 March 2025 (Azure blog 26 February 2025). 5.6B parameters. MIT licence on the HF header. Speech LoRA is about 460M parameters inside that 5.6B, per the technical report. Audio languages: English, Chinese, German, French, Italian, Japanese, Spanish, Portuguese. Text covers a longer list (including Arabic, Czech, Danish, Dutch, Finnish, Hebrew, Hungarian, Korean, Norwegian, Polish, Russian, Swedish, Thai, Turkish, Ukrainian) but those are text, not audio. Vision is English. 128K context. ONNX is linked for Phi-4-mini-instruct, not stated as the speech model’s shipping format. — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-multimodal-instruct); [Azure blog](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/empowering-innovation-the-next-generation-of-the-phi-family/); [technical report HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01743v1)
- Author claims: ranks first on the HF Open ASR leaderboard with WER 6.14% vs previous best 6.5% as of 4 March 2025 / “as of February 2025”; “surpassing expert ASR model WhisperV3 and ST models SeamlessM4T-v2-Large.” The report’s own table lists OpenASR WER 6.14 for Phi-4-Multimodal 5.6B vs WhisperV3 7.44. The ASR prompt quoted is “Transcribe the audio clip into text,” and the authors say it recognizes the eight languages without a language hint. — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-multimodal-instruct); [report v2 table discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01743v2)
- Later leaderboard, 27 March 2026, five-language average: Phi-4 Multimodal Instruct WER 4.41, RTFx 78.2 vs Whisper large-v3 4.81 / 111. Per language: DE 3.96, FR 5.20, IT 4.15, ES 3.71, PT 5.12. More accurate than Whisper on this average, slower RTFx than Whisper. — [Interspeech 2026 PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/srivastav26_interspeech.pdf)
- There is no separate small “Phi-4-mini audio” recognizer in these sources. Phi-4-multimodal is the speech-capable model; Phi-4-mini is the text model it extends. A Korean fine-tune is documented as an appendix example, not as shipped multilingual coverage. — [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01743v1)

#### IBM Granite Speech (2025, with a later family on the docs site)

- Granite Speech 3.3 8B announced 16 April 2025, Apache 2.0, on top of Granite 3.3 Instruct. Initial technical description: English ASR plus English-to-X speech translation into French, Spanish, Italian, German, Portuguese, Japanese, and Chinese. The 16 April post says the audio encoder was English-only at that point. Marketing claim: “consistently delivers greater accuracy than leading open and closed model competitors” and, in an IBM employee post, “Outperforms Whisper, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash on key benchmarks.” No numeric WER was extracted from the IBM post. — [IBM announcement](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/ibm-granite-3-3-speech-recognition-refined-reasoning-rag-loras); [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08699v2)
- Card text reproduced by CodeSOTA for revision 3.3.2, release date stated there as 19 June 2025: multilingual speech input in English, French, German, Spanish, and Portuguese; speech translation to and from English for those languages plus English-to-Japanese and English-to-Mandarin. Apache 2.0. 2B and 8B variants exist (`granite-speech-3.3-2b`, `granite-speech-3.3-8b`). — [CodeSOTA card mirror](https://www.codesota.com/model/granite-speech-3-3-8b)
- IBM Granite docs page (fetched this pass, docs timestamp not a model date) adds a later suite: `granite-speech-5.0-470m-turboctc` compact English ASR “well suited for deployment on laptops, smartphones and other edge devices”; `granite-speech-4.1-2b` ASR and AST with “improved punctuation and capitalization” for English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese, and Japanese; `granite-speech-4.1-2b-plus` adds speaker-attributed ASR, timestamps, and keyword-prompted ASR; `granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar` is a non-autoregressive faster variant. Apache 2.0. Release dates for 4.1 and 5.0 were not on the extracted page. — [IBM Granite Speech docs](https://www.ibm.com/granite/docs/models/speech)
- A third-party aggregator (LLMap, written 12 Oct 2026) says Granite Speech 3.3 2B is ~3B-class, Apache 2.0, languages en/fr/de/es/pt, and quotes Open ASR Leaderboard speed “509×”. Another aggregator (CodeSOTA) says the 8B has 5.74% mean WER. Those numbers were not checked against IBM’s tables. — [LLMap](https://llmap.ai/models/ibm-granite-granite-speech-3-3-2b); [CodeSOTA](https://www.codesota.com/model/granite-speech-3-3-8b)

#### Google Gemma 3n (2025) — on-device multimodal with audio; language list not published in sources read

- Full open-weight release 26 June 2025. E2B and E4B. Raw parameter counts about 5B and 8B, effective sizes 2B and 4B, “as little as 2GB (E2B) and 3GB (E4B) of memory.” Audio encoder based on the Universal Speech Model; one token per 160 ms. Inputs: image, audio, video, text. Outputs: text. Authors: ASR and AST on device; “particularly strong AST results” between English and Spanish, French, Italian, and Portuguese. Text in over 140 languages; “35 languages for multimodal understanding.” The 35 are not listed in the pages read. HF pipeline tags on `google/gemma-3n-E4B` include automatic-speech-recognition and automatic-speech-translation. Trained on ~11 trillion tokens, knowledge cutoff June 2024. — [Google developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-gemma-3n-developer-guide/); [DeepMind product page](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-3n/); [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/gemma3n); [gemma-3n-E4B card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3n-E4B)
- Simon Willison, 26 June 2025, reports a 15.74 GB bfloat16 download of an E4B MLX build and a successful English transcription. That is one user’s download size, not Google’s quoted footprint. — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/26/gemma-3n/)
- A Google Gemma cookbook notebook titles audio support as “Gemma 4” and names `google/gemma-4-E2B-it`, `gemma-4-E4B-it`, and `gemma-4-12B-it` as “trained for multilingual speech recognition.” No language inventory, parameter table, licence, or release date was retrieved for Gemma 4. Treat as a lead, not a characterized candidate. — [Gemma cookbook Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/google-gemma/cookbook/blob/main/docs/capabilities/audio.ipynb)

#### Useful Sensors / Moonshine (English prior art; six-language flavors in 2025)

- “Flavors of Moonshine,” arXiv:2509.02523, 2 September 2025. Monolingual 27M models for Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Ukrainian, and Vietnamese. Authors: average error rates 48% lower than comparably sized Whisper Tiny, better than the 9× larger Whisper Small, and “in most cases” matching or beating the 28× larger Whisper Medium. HF model cards show base variants at 61.5M parameters (updated December 2025). A Chinese tiny card lists moonshine tiny 27M FLEURS CER 29.44 vs Whisper tiny 39M CER 71.1 and Whisper medium 769M CER 52.13 (medium wins Common Voice 17: 22.17 vs moonshine tiny 36.1). Release date on that card: September 2025. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02523); [moonshine-tiny-zh card](https://hf-mirror.com/UsefulSensors/moonshine-tiny-zh); [UsefulSensors org](https://huggingface.co/UsefulSensors)
- The arXiv abstract says the six-language models are released “under a permissive open-source license.” A downstream GGUF document says the non-English fine-tunes use the Moonshine AI Community License: free for research and non-commercial use, commercial use requires registration and is limited to organizations under US$1M annual revenue, unlike English Moonshine which that document calls MIT. Those two licence descriptions conflict; the HF licence file was not opened. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02523); [transcribe.cpp moonshine doc](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/blob/main/docs/models/moonshine.md)
- Same downstream doc: Moonshine is an encoder-decoder on raw 16 kHz PCM, “transcript-only output (no language tokens, no `<|translate|>`, no timestamps).” Q8_0 sizes it publishes: tiny 27M at 35 MB, base 62M at 77 MB, with its own FLEURS numbers (for example tiny-uk WER 18.76, base-uk 14.38, tiny-vi 13.37, base-vi 9.96). Each fine-tune is single-language, no auto-detect. — [transcribe.cpp moonshine doc](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/blob/main/docs/models/moonshine.md)
- Useful Sensors also lists `moonshine-streaming-tiny`, `-small`, and `-medium` on Hugging Face. A public comment on the org page asks whether a multilingual streaming version is planned. This pass did not open those cards, so language, size, and licence for the streaming line are unknown. English tiny/base Moonshine (about 27M, real-time on device) is prior art relative to the 2025 flavors paper. — [UsefulSensors org](https://huggingface.co/UsefulSensors); [OpenASR moonshine-tiny card](https://huggingface.co/OpenASR/moonshine-tiny)

#### FunASR: SenseVoice, Paraformer, Fun-ASR-Nano / MLT-Nano, GLM-ASR-Nano

- SenseVoice-Small, July 2024 (prior art), FunAudioLLM. HF highlights claim training on 400,000+ hours, “more than 50 languages,” and performance surpassing Whisper, but the same card’s “what’s new” and later FunASR docs describe the released Small checkpoint as Mandarin, Cantonese, English, Japanese, and Korean, with emotion and audio-event tags. Parameter count used by FunASR’s 2026 model index: 234M. Non-autoregressive. ONNX and libtorch export added July 2024. HF licence tag is `model-license` / `other`, not Apache. A 2026 FunASR blog says the “whole FunASR stack is open-source (MIT)” and that SenseVoice commercial use is allowed. Those licence statements were not reconciled by reading the licence file. FunASR’s model page associates “70ms/10s” with SenseVoice. Language ID is emitted as a tag (`<|en|>` and zh/yue/ja/ko). `use_itn=True` is documented as adding punctuation and inverse text normalization. — [SenseVoiceSmall card](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/SenseVoiceSmall); [FunASR English model index](https://www.funasr.com/en/models.html); [SenseVoice deployment guide](https://www.funasr.com/en/blog/sensevoice-deployment-guide.html)
- Paraformer-zh, prior art: about 220M, non-autoregressive, Chinese and Cantonese in the FunASR index (another table on the same site says zh/en). FunASR says built-in punctuation and timestamp prediction, trained on 60,000+ hours of Mandarin. Not a 30-language model. — [FunASR model index](https://www.funasr.com/en/models.html)
- Fun-ASR-Nano, noted in FunASR history as Fun-ASR-Nano-2512 on 15 December 2025: 800M, “31 languages,” timestamps, speaker diarization, hotwords. Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano is a separate 31-language checkpoint. The 31 codes were not listed in pages read. Qwen’s benchmark table shows Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano worse than Whisper-large-v3 on every open set in that table (Fleurs 10.03 vs 5.27, MLS 28.70 vs 8.62). Fun-ASR-Nano (the zh/en/ja flagship, not MLT) is described as SenseVoice encoder plus a Qwen3-0.6B decoder. — [FunASR model index](https://www.funasr.com/en/models.html); [which-model guide](https://www.funasr.com/en/blog/which-funasr-model.html); [Qwen3-ASR README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/)
- GLM-ASR-Nano-2512: FunASR lists it as 1.5B and 17 languages, added in the toolkit on 20 May 2026. Language list not retrieved. On Qwen’s table it is behind Whisper-large-v3 on Fleurs (16.08 vs 5.27) and MLS (13.32 vs 8.62). — [FunASR GitHub tree notes](https://github.com/modelscope/FunASR/tree/2499159c62f65274ce958a449f538794a04c01f8); [Qwen README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/)

#### Kyutai STT (2025) — streaming, English and French only

- Open-sourced 19 June 2025. Two models, weights CC-BY-4.0. `kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr`: about 1B parameters, English and French, 0.5 s delay, semantic VAD. `kyutai/stt-2.6b-en`: about 2.6B, English only, 2.5 s delay. Authors: the 2.6B “beats Whisper Large v3 on benchmarks” while streaming, and can run 400 real-time streams on one H100. Pretraining used 2.5 million hours with Whisper pseudo-labels. — [Kyutai blog](https://kyutai.org/blog/2025-06-19-stt-open-source/); [model card mirror](https://hf-mirror.com/kyutai/stt-1b-en_fr); [Kyutai post](https://x.com/kyutai_labs/status/1935652245737287746)
- Leaderboard 8 Oct 2025: Kyutai STT 2.6B en English average WER 6.40, RTFx 88.37, 1 language. Long-form average WER 6.64, RTFx 52.56, slightly worse WER than Whisper large-v3’s long-form 6.43 and lower RTFx than Whisper’s 68.56 in that table. The “beats Whisper” claim and the leaderboard long-form row are both real and do not say the same thing; the blog does not specify which benchmarks. — [leaderboard paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)

#### Distil-Whisper, LiteASR, CrisperWhisper

- Distil-Whisper (Hugging Face), still English-only on the org card, which also announces a distil-large-v3.5 collection. Org table: distil-large-v3 is 756M params, 6.3× relative latency vs large-v3, short-form WER 9.7 vs large-v3 8.4, long-form 10.8 vs 11.0. distil-small.en is 166M. The org states: “Distil-Whisper is currently only available for English speech recognition.” Leaderboard 8 Oct 2025 lists Distil-Whisper Large v3.5 at English WER 7.21, RTFx 202.03, 1 language, ahead of Whisper large-v3’s 7.44 but behind several 2025 models. No multilingual distillation with shipped weights was found. — [distil-whisper org](https://huggingface.co/distil-whisper); [leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)
- LiteASR, arXiv:2502.20583, submitted February 2025, revised 23 August 2025. Low-rank compression of Whisper’s encoder. Authors: compressing Whisper large-v3’s encoder by over 50% can match Whisper medium’s size with better accuracy; another operating point is about 40% encoder-size reduction and about 1.4× speedup “with negligible accuracy loss.” Code: https://github.com/efeslab/LiteASR. This pass did not find a Hugging Face weight release, so it is a method-plus-code paper until weights are confirmed. — [LiteASR HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20583v2)
- CrisperWhisper 1.0 (Nyra Health) is on the 8 Oct 2025 leaderboard as English-only: average WER 6.67, RTFx 84.05, 1 language. — [leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)
- CrisperWhisper 2.0, `nyralabs/CrisperWhisper2.0_large`, card dated 21 July 2026. Tags include English and German. Authors call it “the most accurate verbatim speech recognition you can run in production” and say verbatim and intended modes “work across most languages Whisper supports,” with a 10-language disfluency F1 of 87.8 (open) and 93.5 (Pro) on their own benchmark, ahead of ElevenLabs Scribe v2 at 79.2. Sizes shipped as large, turbo, medium, small, plus Pro variants. Licence tag: `nyra-health-non-commercial-research`. Word timestamps and CTranslate2 are named. The non-commercial licence makes this a poor permissive replacement even though the quality claim is multilingual. The ten benchmark languages were not listed in the extract. — [CrisperWhisper 2.0 card](https://huggingface.co/nyralabs/CrisperWhisper2.0_large)

#### MMS, SeamlessM4T, Zipformer / sherpa-onnx (coverage notes, weaker 2025 evidence)

- OWSM v4’s comparison table lists MMS-all as 0.97B parameters and 1,162 languages, and MMS-fl102 as the 102-language FLEURS setup. OWSM-CTC v4 is described as similar in FLEURS average to MMS-all (10.7% vs 10.6%) and 20% faster, and better than MMS-all on 13 of 21 highlighted languages. MMS and SeamlessM4T model cards were not re-opened here, so licence, punctuation, timestamps, and LID behavior are not restated. SeamlessM4T-v2-large is the large model Canary and Phi claim to match or beat on AST/ASR; its size is given as 2.3B in the Phi report’s comparison header. Both are prior art (2023) relative to this survey. — [OWSM v4 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00338); [Phi-4 report table header](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01743v2)
- No 2025–2026 Zipformer / icefall model covering the 30-language set was found. sherpa-onnx is a runtime that packages other models (Whisper, SenseVoice, Paraformer, Moonshine) and ONNX exports; that is a deployment fact and was not researched further. Parakeet and SenseVoice do have community or official ONNX/GGUF paths noted above.

#### What the leaderboard says is ahead of Whisper, without a model card in this pass

- As of 27 March 2026 the leaderboard had 86 models, 74 open-source, from 26 organizations. Closed models occupied the top of the five-language track (ElevenLabs Scribe v2 average WER 2.67, AssemblyAI Universal 3 Pro 3.23, Speechmatics Enhanced 4.29). — [Interspeech 2026 PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/srivastav26_interspeech.pdf)
- On 15 October 2026 Otter.ai said its in-house ASR was first on the English Open ASR Leaderboard, ahead of Microsoft, Zoom, NVIDIA, and IBM. Weights were not offered. — [Otter post](https://x.com/otter_ai/status/2110827641741906204)
- A 23 October 2026 benchmark mirror listed Edge0/ARK-ASR-3B at 4.76 as the open-asr-leaderboard number-one, 63 entries. No model card, licence, or language list was retrieved. Do not treat it as a candidate. — [QuantID mirror](https://quantid-measurement-lab.static.hf.space/a/hf-official-benchmarks-daily-2026-10-23.html)

### Inferences

- For a single on-device model that must accept whichever of the 30 languages the user speaks, the only open-weight families whose authors claim coverage wide enough are Omnilingual ASR (1,600+) and OWSM v4 (151). Qwen3-ASR is the better accuracy/size story if the product can drop Ukrainian, Norwegian, Hebrew, Catalan, and Croatian. NVIDIA Parakeet-0.6B-v3 is the better speed/size story if the product can drop everything outside those 25 European languages.
- Voxtral Small 24B wins the public five-language open track in the March 2026 snapshot and is the wrong size class for on-device voice notes. Voxtral Mini 3B is closer to “medium/large” and only covers eight languages. Voxtral 3 would change this conclusion if weights were released; they were not, as of the 22 October 2026 review.
- “Beats Whisper” on the HF leaderboard almost always means English, or de/fr/it/es/pt. It is not evidence about Ukrainian, Hebrew, Thai, Hindi, or Chinese.
- Phi-4-multimodal, Granite Speech, Kyutai, Distil-Whisper, Moonshine flavors, SenseVoice, and Paraformer each replace Whisper only inside a much smaller language set. They are competitors for English or for a handful of languages, not successors for this 30-language matrix.
- CrisperWhisper 2.0 is technically interesting for verbatim voice notes and claims Whisper-like language coverage, but the published licence tag is non-commercial research, so it is not a drop-in open replacement.

### Gaps

- Omnilingual ASR: per-code confirmation against `lang_ids.py` was not done. Punctuation, word timestamps, and whether language ID is automatic or must be passed in were not in the pages read. Quantized download sizes were not found (only FP32 encoder sizes).
- Qwen3-ASR: quantized file sizes from the official repo were not opened. Punctuation behavior is not spelled out (timestamps are delegated to the 11-language forced aligner; which 11 was not copied). The January 2026 paper/repo date and a secondary June 2026 HF-card date were not reconciled.
- Canary-1B-v2: download size in GB, and whether it emits punctuation, timestamps, and automatic language ID, were not on the abstract. The 8.1% vs 9.9% multilingual WER figure is from a paper-summary site, not the abstract extract.
- OWSM v4: licence not read; the 151 language codes were not listed, so the gaps among the 30 are unverified. Chinese is called out by the authors as a weaker case (“higher error rates for certain languages, such as Chinese”) without a number in the extract.
- Gemma 3n: the 35 multimodal languages are unknown. Gemma licence text was not quoted. Gemma 4 ASR, if the cookbook is current, is unclassified.
- Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano’s 31 languages, GLM-ASR-Nano’s 17 languages, and CrisperWhisper 2.0’s ten benchmark languages were not listed.
- LiteASR weights: code is public; a downloadable compressed checkpoint was not confirmed.
- Moondream Parakeet Ultra: primary model card not read.
- MMS and SeamlessM4T licences and feature flags (punctuation, timestamps, LID) were not re-read. MMS language count used here is the OWSM paper’s 1,162, not a fresh MMS card.
- Whisper’s own per-code list was not re-downloaded. The leaderboard records 99 languages. Norwegian in Whisper is the code `no`, not `nb`.
- ARK-ASR-3B and Otter’s English leaderboard winner: no weights or language lists found.
- Parameter counts for Whisper large-v3-turbo (often cited elsewhere as 809M) were not in the sources read this pass. Only the 32-to-4 decoder-layer reduction was sourced.

## Which of the 30 languages does each candidate support?

### Takeaway

Whisper’s 99-language set is still the only widely evaluated model in this survey that is generally treated as covering every code in the target list. Among 2025–2026 open models with an explicit list, Qwen3-ASR covers 25 of the 30 and misses uk, nb, he, ca, and hr. Parakeet/Canary v2 cover 19 of the 30, including the often-missed European codes uk, hr, el, hu, and ro, and miss tr, ar, hi, ja, ko, zh, nb, he, id, th, vi, and ca. Phi-4 audio, Granite 3.3, Voxtral 1.0, Kyutai, SenseVoice, and Moonshine flavors each miss most of the list. Omnilingual ASR and OWSM v4 claim 1,600+ and 151 languages respectively, which should include this set, but the actual code lists were not audited here.

### Inferences

- The gaps the brief called out (uk, nb, ca, hr, he, th, vi, el, hu, ro) are not uniformly hard. Parakeet/Canary cover uk, hr, el, hu, ro and miss nb, ca, he, th, vi. Qwen3-ASR covers th, vi, el, hu, ro and misses uk, nb, ca, hr, he. A two-model on-device design could cover more of the 30 than either model alone (Qwen3-ASR plus Parakeet would still miss nb, he, and ca, and would miss hr only if Qwen is used for the languages it owns; hr is in Parakeet). That is an inference about set union, not a tested system.
- Norwegian Bokmål (`nb`) is a real hole for both Qwen3-ASR and Parakeet/Canary. Whisper’s code is `no`. Voxtral 3’s unpublished weights are the only 2026 system in this survey whose printed language list includes NO.
- Hebrew is unsupported by every explicit 2025–2026 list retrieved except whatever sits inside Omnilingual’s 1,600, OWSM’s 151, CrisperWhisper’s “most Whisper languages,” and possibly Gemma’s unnamed 35. Phi-4’s Hebrew support is text, not audio.
- Catalan is in the Voxtral 3 figure and in Whisper’s historical set, and is absent from Qwen3-ASR and Parakeet.
- Single-language Moonshine checkpoints do not add up to a multilingual voice-note model: there is no auto language ID, and 24 of the 30 codes have no flavor.

### Gaps

- Omnilingual `lang_ids.py` and OWSM’s 151-code list were not opened, so it is not proven here that every target code is present. Given the headline counts, a total miss of a major language would be surprising, but Ukrainian vs a related code, Norwegian Bokmål vs Nynorsk, and Chinese script variants were not checked.
- Fun-ASR 31, GLM-ASR 17, Gemma 3n 35, and CrisperWhisper’s ten languages are unknown.
- Whether Canary-1B-v2’s 25 languages are exactly Parakeet’s 25 is taken from the paper’s phrase “the same 25 languages,” not from a Canary card with the codes spelled out.
- SeamlessM4T v2 speech-input languages were not re-listed.
- Voxtral 3’s star on VI, and whether he/hr exist outside the figure, were not explained in the extract.

## What do the authors claim versus Whisper on speed, size, and word error rate?

### Takeaway

Author claims that are both numeric and tied to a paper or model card: Canary-1B-v2 is about 10× faster than Whisper large-v3 on English and slightly more accurate on the HF English leaderboard (7.15% vs 7.44% WER; RTFx 749 vs 145.51); Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 is much faster still (English WER 6.32%, RTFx about 3333) at 600M parameters; Qwen3-ASR-1.7B beats Whisper-large-v3 on five of six open rows in Qwen’s table and loses one Fleurs slice; Phi-4-multimodal reported 6.14% vs WhisperV3 7.44% on OpenASR in early 2025; OWSM v4 medium reports 9.4% vs Whisper-medium 9.7% average WER; Moonshine-27M reports large gains versus Whisper Tiny and Small. Several “beats Whisper” sentences from IBM, Kyutai, SenseVoice, and Voxtral are real author claims without a full comparable table in the pages read. Leaderboard RTFx is not a phone benchmark.

### Inferences

- On public English leaderboard numbers from October 2025, several open models already had lower average WER than Whisper large-v3 (7.44): Parakeet v3 (6.32), Canary Flash (6.35), Kyutai 2.6B en (6.40), Canary 1B (6.50), CrisperWhisper (6.67), Voxtral Mini 3B (7.05), Canary-1B-v2 (7.15), Distil-Whisper v3.5 (7.21). Only Parakeet v3, Canary-1B-v2, and Voxtral among those are multilingual, and only the first two are small enough to resemble a Whisper-large replacement rather than an LLM.
- Speed leadership on that same table belongs to FastConformer/TDT and CTC models (Parakeet v3 RTFx 3332, Parakeet CTC 1.1B 2728), not to LLM ASR. Omnilingual 7B and Voxtral 24B buy accuracy on the five-language track by spending RTFx (about 21 and 42 versus Whisper’s 111).
- For on-device size, the sourced footprints that are actually in Whisper-small/medium range are Moonshine (27–62M, tens of MB quantized), OWSM base/small (102M/370M), Granite’s 470M English turbo, Parakeet 600M (about 0.5 GB at Q4_K in a community pack, 2.5 GB unquantized), Distil-Whisper 166M–756M English, and Qwen3-ASR-0.6B (sub-1B class; official byte size not confirmed). Phi-4 at 5.6B, Voxtral 24B, Omnilingual 7B, and Gemma 3n E4B raw weights are large-v3-class or bigger.
- Author WER claims are not interchangeable. Moonshine’s 48% relative gain is versus Whisper Tiny, and on at least one Chinese set Whisper Medium still wins. Canary’ s 10× is English RTFx, not a 30-language WER. Qwen’s Fleurs win coexists with a Fleurs subset loss.

### Gaps

- No author in the sources published a WER table on the full 30-language set against Whisper small, medium, large-v3, and large-v3-turbo.
- Whisper large-v3-turbo’s own leaderboard WER and RTFx were not extracted.
- Official quantized sizes are missing for Qwen3-ASR, Canary-1B-v2, Voxtral 3B/24B, Phi-4, Granite 8B/2B, OWSM, and Omnilingual LLM checkpoints.
- IBM’s “outperforms Whisper” claim has no number in the sources read. Third-party 5.74% mean WER for Granite 8B was not verified.
- SenseVoice’s “surpasses Whisper” claim has no WER pair in the extract. The older “15× faster than Whisper-Large” line was not present in the extracts and is intentionally omitted.
- Kyutai’s “beats Whisper large v3” benchmarks were not identified, and the long-form leaderboard row goes the other way.
- Gemma 3n has no sourced WER.
- Parakeet Ultra’s WER deltas are one vendor’s Mac measurements, not NVIDIA’s and not the HF leaderboard.
- RTFx in all of these papers is GPU-server throughput. It does not answer on-device latency. That question belongs to the deployment researcher.

## Which candidates are English-only or Europe-only and therefore cannot cover this language set?

### Takeaway

English-only, or English-plus-French, models cannot be the recognizer for this set: Distil-Whisper (including v3.5), Kyutai STT 2.6B, Kyutai STT 1B (English and French only), CrisperWhisper 1.0, NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2, Parakeet CTC 1.1B, and Granite Speech 5.0 470M turbo. Europe-only or “a few European languages plus one or two others” models that still miss the Asian, Middle-Eastern, and some Nordic codes: Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 and Canary-1B-v2 (25 European languages), Canary 1B and Canary 1B Flash (4 languages), Voxtral Mini/Small 2025 (8 languages), Granite Speech 3.3/4.1 (five or six languages), and Phi-4-multimodal audio (eight languages, four of them non-European: en, zh, ja plus five European). SenseVoice-Small and Paraformer are Chinese/East-Asian, not European, and also fail the set. Moonshine flavors cover six non-English languages as separate models and fail the set as a product.

### Cited Findings

- Distil-Whisper org: “currently only available for English speech recognition.” Leaderboard marks distil-large-v3.5 as 1 language. — [org card](https://huggingface.co/distil-whisper); [leaderboard v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)

Canary 1B and Canary 1B Flash are 4 languages on the leaderboard. — [Parakeet v3 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3); [leaderboard v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v1)

Voxtral 3’s language list is broad and includes Asian languages, but the weights are not public.

### Inferences

- A Europe-only model is a functional miss for this product even when its European WER beats Whisper. Parakeet v3 is the clearest case: it is the right size and speed, it covers Ukrainian, Croatian, Greek, Hungarian, and Romanian, and it still cannot transcribe Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Turkish, Norwegian, or Catalan.
- English-only efficiency work (Distil-Whisper, LiteASR, Parakeet v2, Granite 470M) can replace Whisper only for English voice notes. LiteASR is a compression of Whisper, so a LiteASR-compressed multilingual Whisper would be a different artifact; the paper demonstrates the method on Whisper large-v3 and does not say a 99-language compressed checkpoint was released.
- The cloud APIs below are out of scope for on-device weights even when they top the leaderboard.

### Gaps

- Canary 1B and Canary 1B Flash: the four language codes were not re-read. They are treated as a small set because the leaderboard count is 4, not because this pass printed the codes.
- Gemma 3n could turn out to be Europe-heavy. The blog highlights EN↔ES/FR/IT/PT AST and does not publish the other 31 multimodal languages.
- Whether any sherpa-onnx Zipformer checkpoint added a broad multilingual model in 2025–2026 was not established. The absence of a hit is not a proof that none exists.

### Gaps
